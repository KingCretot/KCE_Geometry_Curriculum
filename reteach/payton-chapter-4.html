Part 1 · The Toolbox

Five shortcuts that work, two that never do

SSSAll three pairs of sides.
SASTwo sides and the angle between them.
ASATwo angles and the side between them.
AASTwo angles and a side that is not between them.
HLRight triangles only: hypotenuse + one leg.
SSA ✗Two sides and an angle not between them. The third side can swing into two positions.
AAA ✗Same shape, not always the same size (think photo vs. poster).

The key word: "included"

An angle is included between two sides when its vertex is the point the two sides share. A side is included between two angles when the side's two endpoints are the two angles' vertices. Use the letters:

The four-step method (use it on every problem)

  1. Mark what you know. Copy every given onto the picture, then add the free facts: a shared side (Reflexive Property), vertical angles, right-angle marks.
  2. Count. How many pairs of sides (S) and angles (A) do you have?
  3. Walk around the triangle. Go A → B → C and say each marked part in order. Is the angle sandwiched between the two sides? Is the side sandwiched between the two angles?
  4. Name the shortcut, or say "not enough" if what you have is SSA or AAA.
Worked example. Given ∠A ≅ ∠D, ∠B ≅ ∠E, and BC ≅ EF.
  1. Mark: angle at A, angle at B, side BC.
  2. Count: 2 angles + 1 side, so it's ASA or AAS.
  3. Walk: the side between ∠A and ∠B would be AB. Our side is BC, whose endpoints are B and C, and A is not one of them. So the side is not included.
  4. Two angles + non-included side = AAS. ✓

Part 4 · Right triangles and HL (Lesson 4.6)

The one time "SSA" is allowed

Hypotenuse = the side across from the right angle (always the longest). The other two sides are the legs.

HL Theorem needs all three: (1) both triangles have a marked right angle, (2) the hypotenuses are congruent, (3) one pair of legs is congruent.

Other right-triangle cases use the regular shortcuts: both legs → SAS (the right angle is between the legs); hypotenuse + an acute angle → AAS; a leg + an acute angle → ASA or AAS (walk around to see which).

Part 5 · Matching parts, Reflexive, CPCTC (Lessons 4.2–4.5)

Order matters

In ΔJKL ≅ ΔRST the letters match by position: 1st ↔ 1st, 2nd ↔ 2nd, 3rd ↔ 3rd. So J↔R, K↔S, L↔T, and side KL ↔ side ST (2nd-3rd ↔ 2nd-3rd).

Reflexive Property: a shared side is congruent to itself (BD ≅ BD). CPCTC (Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Congruent) can only be used after the triangles are proven congruent.

Part 6 · Angles in triangles (Lesson 4.1)

180° and the exterior angle

Angle sum: the three interior angles add to 180°. Exterior angle: equals the sum of the two remote interior angles (the two not touching it). Type numbers only, no ° sign needed.

Part 7 · Triangles on the coordinate plane (Lesson 4.7)

Distance for sides, slope for right angles

Sides: Distance Formula d = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²). Two equal sides → isosceles; all different → scalene. Right angle: two sides with slopes whose product is −1, or one horizontal side meeting one vertical side.
