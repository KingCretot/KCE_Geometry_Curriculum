Let's Begin

One altitude, three similar triangles

Draw the altitude from the right angle of a right triangle to its hypotenuse, and something remarkable happens: you get three triangles, and all three are similar. That fact leads to a fast way to find missing lengths using the geometric mean.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Find the geometric mean of two numbers
  • 2Explain why the altitude to the hypotenuse creates three similar triangles
  • 3Use the geometric mean relationships to find missing lengths

Vocabulary

The geometric mean

The geometric mean of two positive numbers a and b is the positive number x where a/x = x/b. Solving gives x² = ab, so x = √(ab).

1
Example
The geometric mean of 4 and 9 is √36 = 6. Check: 4/6 = 6/9 ✓

See It

The altitude to the hypotenuse

RIGHT △ABC WITH ALTITUDE CD

CD splits the hypotenuse into segments of 9 and 16.

△ACB, △ADC, and △CDB are all similar. Each small triangle shares an acute angle with the big one, and all three have a right angle, so AA applies to each pair.

Two Relationships

Altitude and legs as geometric means

Altitude Rule
h = √(segment × segment)

The altitude is the geometric mean of the two pieces of the hypotenuse.

Leg Rule
leg = √(hypotenuse × adjacent segment)

Each leg is the geometric mean of the whole hypotenuse and the piece of the hypotenuse right next to that leg.

Worked Example

Using the diagram above

Problem: The hypotenuse is split into AD = 9 and DB = 16. Find the altitude h and both legs.

1
Altitude
h = √(9 × 16) = √144 = 12
2
Leg AC
The hypotenuse is 25 and the piece next to AC is 9: AC = √(25 × 9) = √225 = 15
3
Leg BC
BC = √(25 × 16) = √400 = 20
4
Check with Pythagoras
15² + 20² = 225 + 400 = 625 = 25² ✓

Watch Out

Use the adjacent segment

For a leg, multiply the whole hypotenuse by the segment that touches that leg, not the far one.

For AC above, the segment that touches A is AD = 9. Using 16 instead would give 20, which is the other leg.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Use the geometric mean.

Question 1 — What is the geometric mean of 4 and 16?

Here's why: √(4 × 16) = √64 = 8. 10 is the arithmetic mean (the ordinary average), and 64 forgets the square root.

Question 2 — The altitude to the hypotenuse splits it into segments of 2 and 8. How long is the altitude?

Here's why: h = √(2 × 8) = √16 = 4.

Why This Matters

Surveying and architecture

Surveyors and carpenters use the geometric mean relationships to find heights and distances when only part of a right triangle can be measured, for instance the two pieces of a roof’s span on either side of a support post.

Real world: the geometric mean also shows up in finance and science. An investment that grows 4× one year and 9× the next grew the same as one that grew 6× each year, since 4 × 9 = 6 × 6.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
What is the geometric mean of 3 and 27?
Here's why: √(3 × 27) = √81 = 9. 15 is the ordinary average.
Question 2
The altitude to the hypotenuse splits it into segments of 3 and 12. How long is the altitude?
Here's why: h = √(3 × 12) = √36 = 6. 36 forgets the square root.
Question 3
A right triangle’s hypotenuse is 25. The segment next to one leg is 9. How long is that leg?
Here's why: leg = √(25 × 9) = √225 = 15. 20 uses the far segment, 16.
Question 4
Counting the original, how many similar triangles does the altitude to the hypotenuse create?
Here's why: The original triangle and the two smaller ones it is split into: three triangles, all similar.
Question 5 — True or False
The geometric mean of 5 and 20 is 12.5.
Here's why: False. √(5 × 20) = √100 = 10. 12.5 is the ordinary average.
