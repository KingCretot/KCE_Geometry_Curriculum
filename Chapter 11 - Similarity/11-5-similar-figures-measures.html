Let's Begin

Double the length, quadruple the area

If two figures are similar with a scale factor of 2, every length doubles. But the area doesn’t double; it quadruples. And for solids, the volume grows eight times. This lesson shows the pattern behind those surprising jumps.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1State how perimeter, area, and volume change with scale factor
  • 2Find a missing area or volume from a similar figure
  • 3Work backward from an area or volume ratio to the scale factor

The Big Rule

Lengths, areas, volumes

If the scale factor is a : b, then...Ratio
Lengths (sides, perimeters, heights)a : b
Areas (and surface areas)a² : b²
Volumesa³ : b³

Area is two-dimensional, so the scale factor is used twice. Volume is three-dimensional, so it is used three times.

See It

Why area squares

A 1 × 1 SQUARE SCALED BY 3

Each side triples, so the big square holds 3 × 3 = 9 copies of the small one.

Worked Example

Finding a missing area

Problem: Two similar triangles have a scale factor of 2 : 3. The smaller one has an area of 20 cm². Find the area of the larger one.

1
Area ratio
2² : 3² = 4 : 9
2
Proportion
4/9 = 20/A, so 4A = 180
3
Answer
A = 45 cm². Notice that 45 is not 30, which is what you’d get by using 2 : 3 directly.

Worked Example

Volumes, and working backward

Problem: (a) Two cubes have edges in the ratio 1 : 4. Compare their volumes. (b) Two similar figures have areas in the ratio 16 : 25. Find the scale factor.

1
(a) Cube the scale factor
1³ : 4³ = 1 : 64. The big cube holds 64 small cubes.
2
(b) Undo the square
√16 : √25 = 4 : 5

To go backward from volumes, take the cube root: a volume ratio of 8 : 27 means a scale factor of 2 : 3.

Watch Out

Match the power to the measurement

Before you set up a proportion, ask: is this measurement a length, an area, or a volume? Perimeter is a length, so it uses the plain scale factor. Surface area is an area, so it uses the square.

Real-world check: a pizza 16 inches across has 4 times the area of an 8-inch pizza, not twice, so it should cost far more than twice as much.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Pick the right power.

Question 1 — Two similar figures have a scale factor of 1 : 3. What is the ratio of their areas?

Here's why: Square the scale factor for areas: 1² : 3² = 1 : 9. 1 : 27 would be the volume ratio.

Question 2 — Two similar solids have a scale factor of 2 : 5. What is the ratio of their volumes?

Here's why: Cube the scale factor for volumes: 2³ : 5³ = 8 : 125. 4 : 25 is the area ratio.

Why This Matters

Why giants can’t exist

If a person were scaled up 10 times, their weight (a volume) would grow 1,000 times, but the strength of their bones (based on cross-section area) would grow only 100 times. Their legs would break. This is why elephants have such thick legs and why ants can lift many times their weight.

Real world: engineers testing a 1:50 scale model of a ship must account for this. The model’s hull area is 1/2,500 of the real one, but its volume is only 1/125,000.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
Two similar triangles have a scale factor of 3 : 4. The smaller one has a perimeter of 24. Find the perimeter of the larger one.
Here's why: Perimeter is a length, so use 3 : 4 directly: 3/4 = 24/P gives P = 32. 42.7 wrongly squares the ratio.
Question 2
Two similar figures have a scale factor of 2 : 5. What is the ratio of their areas?
Here's why: Square both parts: 2² : 5² = 4 : 25. 4 : 10 squares only one part.
Question 3
A small figure has an area of 12. A similar figure is larger by a scale factor of 3. What is its area?
Here's why: Area grows by 3² = 9: 12 × 9 = 108. 36 uses the plain scale factor.
Question 4
Two similar solids have volumes in the ratio 8 : 27. What is their scale factor?
Here's why: Take the cube root: ∛8 = 2 and ∛27 = 3. 4 : 9 would be their area ratio.
Question 5 — True or False
Doubling every edge of a cube doubles its volume.
Here's why: False. The volume grows by 2³ = 8.
