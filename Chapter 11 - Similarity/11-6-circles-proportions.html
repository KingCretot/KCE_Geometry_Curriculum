Let's Begin

Products of segments in a circle

When chords, secants, and tangents meet, the pieces they form follow surprisingly neat rules. Each rule comes from a pair of similar triangles, and each one lets you find a missing length with a single multiplication.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Use the products of intersecting chords
  • 2Use the products of two secants from an outside point
  • 3Use the tangent-secant relationship

Rule 1

Two chords that cross inside the circle

a · b = c · d

The two pieces of one chord multiply to the same product as the two pieces of the other.

Problem: One chord is split into 3 and 8. The other is split into 4 and x. Find x.

1
Multiply the pieces
3 · 8 = 4 · x
2
Solve
24 = 4x, so x = 6

Why It Works

Hidden similar triangles

Draw segments connecting the chord endpoints. The two triangles formed have a pair of vertical angles, and a pair of inscribed angles that intercept the same arc (Lesson 8.4), so they are similar by AA. Cross-multiplying their proportion gives a · b = c · d.

This is the payoff of Chapter 11: one similarity argument becomes a rule you can use in one line.

Rule 2

Two secants from an outside point

A secant is a line that crosses a circle at two points. When two secants are drawn from the same outside point:

Secant–Secant
(outside piece) × (whole secant) = (outside piece) × (whole secant)

Problem: One secant has an outside piece of 4 and a whole length of 10. The other has an outside piece of 5. Find its whole length.

1
Multiply
4 · 10 = 5 · w
2
Solve
40 = 5w, so w = 8

Rule 3

A tangent and a secant from an outside point

t² = outside × whole

The tangent segment t touches the circle at one point. The secant passes through it.

Problem: From point P, a secant has an outside piece of 4 and a whole length of 9. Find the tangent segment t.

1
Use the rule
t² = 4 · 9 = 36
2
Solve
t = 6

Watch Out

Whole, not inside

For secants and tangents, multiply the outside piece by the whole secant (outside + inside), not by the inside piece alone.

Chords are the exception: there, you multiply the two pieces of each chord, because the crossing point is inside the circle.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Pick the right rule.

Question 1 — Two chords cross. One is split into 2 and 12, and the other into 3 and x. Find x.

Here's why: 2 · 12 = 3x, so 24 = 3x and x = 8.

Question 2 — A secant from P has an outside piece of 3 and a whole length of 12. How long is the tangent segment from P?

Here's why: t² = 3 · 12 = 36, so t = 6. √27 multiplies by the inside piece, 9, instead of the whole.

Why This Matters

Finding a circle you can only see part of

Archaeologists who find a piece of a broken round plate can measure a chord across it and the height of the arc above that chord. The intersecting chords rule then gives the diameter of the whole plate.

Real world: the tangent-secant rule explains how far you can see to the horizon. Standing at a height h above Earth, the distance to the horizon d satisfies d² = h(h + Earth’s diameter).

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
Two chords cross. One is split into 5 and 6, and the other into 3 and x. Find x.
Here's why: 5 · 6 = 3x, so 30 = 3x and x = 10. 8 adds instead of multiplying.
Question 2
From point P, a secant has an outside piece of 2 and a whole length of 18. How long is the tangent from P?
Here's why: t² = 2 · 18 = 36, so t = 6. 36 forgets the square root.
Question 3
Two secants come from the same point. One has an outside piece of 3 and a whole length of 12. The other has an outside piece of 4. Find its whole length.
Here's why: 3 · 12 = 4w, so 36 = 4w and w = 9.
Question 4
When two chords intersect inside a circle...
Here's why: a · b = c · d. The chords need not be equal or bisect each other.
Question 5 — True or False
Two tangent segments drawn to a circle from the same outside point are congruent.
Here's why: True. Each one’s square equals the same outside-times-whole product, and Chapter 8 proved it directly.
