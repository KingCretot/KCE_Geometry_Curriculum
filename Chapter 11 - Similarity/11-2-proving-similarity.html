Let's Begin

Shortcuts for similar triangles

Checking three angles and three side ratios every time would be slow. Just as SSS, SAS, and ASA were shortcuts for congruence in Chapter 4, there are three shortcuts for proving two triangles are similar.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Use the AA Similarity Postulate
  • 2Use the SSS and SAS Similarity Theorems
  • 3Choose the right shortcut for a pair of triangles

Shortcut 1 · Postulate

AA Similarity

AA Similarity Postulate: if two angles of one triangle are congruent to two angles of another triangle, the triangles are similar.

Why only two? The angles of every triangle add to 180°, so once two pairs match, the third pair has to match as well. AA is by far the most-used similarity shortcut.

Shortcut 2 · Theorem

SSS Similarity

SSS Similarity Theorem: if all three pairs of corresponding sides are proportional, the triangles are similar.

Problem: Are triangles with sides 4, 6, 8 and 6, 9, 12 similar?

1
Pair shortest with shortest, and so on
6/4 = 1.5, 9/6 = 1.5, 12/8 = 1.5
2
Conclude
All three ratios are equal, so the triangles are similar by SSS~.

Shortcut 3 · Theorem

SAS Similarity

SAS Similarity Theorem: if two pairs of sides are proportional and the angles between them (the included angles) are congruent, the triangles are similar.

Problem: One triangle has sides 3 and 5 with a 50° angle between them. Another has sides 6 and 10 with a 50° angle between them.

1
Ratios
6/3 = 2 and 10/5 = 2, so the two pairs of sides are proportional.
2
Included angle
Both are 50°. The triangles are similar by SAS~.

Watch Out

Similarity shortcuts vs. congruence shortcuts

SimilarityCongruence look-alike
AAASA or AAS
SSS~ (sides proportional)SSS (sides equal)
SAS~ (sides proportional)SAS (sides equal)

For similarity, sides need only be proportional. For congruence, they must be equal. And just like congruence, there is no SSA shortcut for similarity.

Worked Example

Using the third angle

Problem: △ABC has angles 35° and 80°. △DEF has angles 35° and 65°. Are they similar?

1
Find each third angle
△ABC: 180 − 35 − 80 = 65°. △DEF: 180 − 35 − 65 = 80°.
2
Compare
Both triangles have angles 35°, 65°, and 80°. They are similar by AA, even though the angles given first looked different.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Pick the shortcut, or decide there isn’t one.

Question 1 — One triangle has angles 40° and 75°. Another has angles 40° and 65°. Are they similar?

Here's why: The first triangle’s third angle is 65°. So both triangles have 40°, 65°, and 75°, and AA applies.

Question 2 — Are triangles with sides 5, 7, 9 and 10, 14, 20 similar?

Here's why: 10/5 = 2 and 14/7 = 2, but 20/9 ≈ 2.22. One ratio is different, so the triangles are not similar.

Why This Matters

Measuring what you can’t reach

Over 2,500 years ago, Thales is said to have found the height of an Egyptian pyramid by comparing its shadow to the shadow of a stick. The sun’s rays strike both at the same angle, and both stand at right angles to the ground, so the two triangles are similar by AA.

Real world: a 6-foot person casts a 4-foot shadow while a tree casts a 30-foot shadow. By AA, 6/4 = h/30, so the tree is 45 feet tall.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
What do you need to prove two triangles similar by AA?
Here's why: AA needs two angle pairs. The third pair then matches automatically.
Question 2
Triangles with sides 3, 4, 5 and 9, 12, 15 are...
Here's why: 9/3 = 12/4 = 15/5 = 3, so all three pairs of sides are proportional.
Question 3
SAS Similarity requires two pairs of proportional sides and...
Here's why: The congruent angles must be the ones between the proportional sides, just like SAS for congruence.
Question 4
One triangle has angles 50° and 60°. Another has angles 50° and 70°. Are they similar?
Here's why: The first triangle’s third angle is 70°, and the second’s is 60°. Both have 50°, 60°, and 70°.
Question 5 — True or False
Any two equilateral triangles are similar.
Here's why: True. Every equilateral triangle has three 60° angles, so AA always applies.
