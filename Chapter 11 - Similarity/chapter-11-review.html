Key Ideas · Lessons 11.1–11.2

Similar figures and triangles

Lesson 11.1

Same angles, proportional sides

Polygons are similar when corresponding angles are congruent and corresponding sides are proportional. The ratio of corresponding sides is the scale factor. Congruent figures are similar with scale factor 1.

Example: △ABC ~ △DEF with AB = 6, DE = 9: scale factor 3/2, so BC = 8 gives EF = 12.
Lesson 11.2

AA, SSS~, SAS~

AA: two pairs of congruent angles. SSS~: all three side ratios equal. SAS~: two side ratios equal and the included angles congruent.

Example: Angles 40° and 75° vs. 40° and 65°: both triangles are 40°–65°–75°, so similar by AA.

Key Ideas · Lessons 11.3–11.4

Proportions inside figures

Lesson 11.3

Geometric mean in right triangles

The altitude to the hypotenuse makes three similar triangles. Altitude = √(segment × segment). Leg = √(hypotenuse × adjacent segment). The geometric mean of a and b is √(ab).

Example: Segments 4 and 9: altitude = √36 = 6.
Lesson 11.4

Parallel lines cut proportionally

Triangle Proportionality: a line parallel to one side splits the other two sides proportionally (and the converse). Three parallel lines cut transversals proportionally. An angle bisector splits the opposite side in the ratio of the adjacent sides.

Example: DE ∥ BC, AD = 4, DB = 6, AE = 6: 4/6 = 6/x, so x = 9.
DE ∥ BC: AD/DB = AE/EC

Key Ideas · Lessons 11.5–11.7

Scaling, circles, golden ratio

Lesson 11.5

Lengths, areas, volumes

Scale factor a : b → perimeters a : b, areas a² : b², volumes a³ : b³. Work backward by taking a square or cube root.

Example: Scale 2 : 3, smaller area 20 → larger area 20 × 9/4 = 45.
Lesson 11.6

Products in circles

Two chords: (piece)(piece) = (piece)(piece). Two secants from one point: (outside)(whole) = (outside)(whole). Tangent and secant: tangent² = (outside)(whole).

Example: Chord pieces 4 and 9, other chord 6 and x: 6x = 36, x = 6.
Lesson 11.7

The golden ratio

φ = (1 + √5)/2 ≈ 1.618. A golden rectangle's sides are in ratio φ : 1; cutting off a square leaves a smaller golden rectangle. Ratios of neighboring Fibonacci numbers approach φ.

Example: Short side 10 → long side ≈ 16.18.

Watch Out

The mistakes that cost the most points

The mistakeThe fix
Matching sides by position on the pageMatch by the order in the similarity statement, or by the angles they're opposite.
Using SSA or AAS-style reasoning for similarityThe tests are AA, SSS~, and SAS~ (angle between the proportional sides).
Setting up DE ∥ BC as AD/AB = AE/ECKeep it consistent: part/part = part/part (AD/DB = AE/EC), or part/whole = part/whole.
Scaling area by the scale factorArea scales by the square, volume by the cube. Doubling every edge of a cube makes 8 times the volume.
Using the arithmetic meanGeometric mean is √(ab), not (a + b)/2. For 5 and 20: 10, not 12.5.
Secant products with the inside piece onlyMultiply the outside piece by the whole secant.

Practice Test

10 questions, every lesson mixed together

Just like a unit test, these jump between lessons. You get two tries on each question, and every question explains itself after you answer.

Question 1
△ABC ~ △DEF with AB = 6, DE = 9, and BC = 8. What is EF?
Here's why: The scale factor from △ABC to △DEF is 9/6 = 1.5, so EF = 8 × 1.5 = 12. 11 adds 3 instead of multiplying, and 5⅓ applies the factor backward.
Question 2
One triangle has angles of 40° and 75°. Another has angles of 40° and 65°. Are they similar?
Here's why: The first triangle's third angle is 180° − 40° − 75° = 65°, so both triangles are 40°–65°–75°. Two pairs of congruent angles prove similarity by AA.
Question 3
Triangles with sides 4, 6, 8 and 6, 9, 12 are…
Here's why: 6/4 = 9/6 = 12/8 = 1.5. All three ratios match, so the triangles are similar by SSS~. They aren't congruent because the sides aren't equal.
Question 4
What is the geometric mean of 4 and 25?
Here's why: √(4 × 25) = √100 = 10. 14.5 is the ordinary average, and 100 forgets the square root.
Question 5
The altitude to the hypotenuse of a right triangle splits it into segments of 4 and 9. How long is the altitude?
Here's why: The altitude is the geometric mean of the two segments: √(4 × 9) = √36 = 6.
Question 6
In △ABC, DE ∥ BC, AD = 4, DB = 6, and AE = 6. What is EC?
Here's why: Triangle Proportionality: AD/DB = AE/EC → 4/6 = 6/EC → 4·EC = 36 → EC = 9.
Question 7
In a triangle, the sides next to an angle are 8 and 12. The bisector of that angle splits the opposite side, which is 15. How long is the piece next to the side of 8?
Here's why: The pieces are in ratio 8 : 12 = 2 : 3. Split 15 into 2 + 3 = 5 parts of 3: the pieces are 6 and 9, and the 6 is next to the side of 8.
Question 8
Two similar figures have a scale factor of 2 : 3. The smaller has an area of 20. What is the area of the larger?
Here's why: Areas scale by the square: 4 : 9. 20 × 9/4 = 45. 30 uses the scale factor directly, and 67.5 uses the cube (volume) ratio.
Question 9
Two chords intersect inside a circle. One is split into 4 and 9; the other into 6 and x. What is x?
Here's why: Products of the pieces are equal: 4 × 9 = 6x → 36 = 6x → x = 6.
Question 10
A golden rectangle has a short side of 10. About how long is its long side?
Here's why: Long side = φ × short side ≈ 1.618 × 10 = 16.18. 6.18 divides by φ instead, which would make the given side the long one.
