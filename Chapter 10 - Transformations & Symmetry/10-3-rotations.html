Let's Begin

Turning a figure around a point

A rotation turns a figure around a fixed point, the center of rotation, through a given angle. In math, a positive angle means a counterclockwise turn unless the problem says otherwise.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Describe a rotation by its center, angle, and direction
  • 2Rotate points 90°, 180°, and 270° about the origin
  • 3Explain how two reflections over intersecting lines make a rotation

Vocabulary

Center, angle, and direction

Every point of the figure stays the same distance from the center, and every point turns through the same angle. The image is congruent to the pre-image and keeps the same orientation.

Counterclockwise is the direction from the positive x-axis up toward the positive y-axis. A 90° clockwise turn is the same as a 270° counterclockwise turn.

Coordinate Rules

Rotations about the origin (counterclockwise)

Rotate counterclockwise by...Rule
90°(x, y) → (−y, x)
180°(x, y) → (−x, −y)
270°(x, y) → (y, −x)

A 180° rotation is the easiest: both signs flip. For 90°, swap the coordinates and then make the new x the opposite.

A Surprising Connection

Two reflections make a rotation

Reflect a figure over one line, then over a second line that intersects the first. The result is a rotation about the intersection point, by twice the angle between the lines.

Example: reflect over the x-axis, then over the line y = x. Those lines meet at a 45° angle, so the result is a 90° rotation about the origin. Check with (3, 1): it goes to (3, −1), then to (−1, 3), the same answer as the 90° rule.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Rotate about the origin, counterclockwise.

Question 1 — Rotate (2, 5) by 180°.

Here's why: 180° flips both signs: (−2, −5). (−5, 2) is the 90° rotation.

Question 2 — Rotate (4, 1) by 90°.

Here's why: 90° uses (−y, x): (−1, 4). (1, −4) is the 270° rotation.

Why This Matters

Wheels, clocks, and logos

Every turning wheel, ceiling fan, and Ferris wheel is a rotation about its axle. Clock hands rotate clockwise, 6° per minute for the minute hand.

Real world: many company logos and hubcaps are designed to look the same after a rotation, which is the idea behind Lesson 10.5 on symmetry.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
Rotate (1, 4) by 90° counterclockwise about the origin.
Here's why: 90° uses (−y, x): (−4, 1). (4, −1) is 270°, and (−1, −4) is 180°.
Question 2
Rotate (−2, 3) by 180° about the origin.
Here's why: 180° changes both signs: (2, −3).
Question 3
A 90° clockwise rotation is the same as...
Here's why: Going 90° one way lands where 270° the other way lands, since 90 + 270 = 360.
Question 4
Reflecting over two lines that meet at a 40° angle produces a rotation of...
Here's why: The rotation angle is twice the angle between the lines: 2 × 40° = 80°.
Question 5 — True or False
A rotation keeps every distance between points the same.
Here's why: True. A rotation is a rigid motion: the figure turns but does not stretch or shrink.
