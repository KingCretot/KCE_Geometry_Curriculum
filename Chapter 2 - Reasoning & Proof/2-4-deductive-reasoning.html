Let's Begin

Inductive reasoning guesses. Deductive reasoning guarantees.

Lesson 2.1's inductive reasoning gave us conjectures — strong guesses. Today's deductive reasoning is different: when you use it correctly, the conclusion isn't a guess anymore. It's guaranteed, every single time.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Explain the difference between inductive and deductive reasoning
  • 2Apply the Law of Detachment
  • 3Apply the Law of Syllogism

Law 1 of 2

The Law of Detachment

LAW OF DETACHMENT

If a conditional is true, and its hypothesis is true, then its conclusion must be true.

If a shape is a square, then it has four right angles. (true conditional)
This shape is a square. (hypothesis is true)
Therefore: this shape has four right angles.

Law 2 of 2

The Law of Syllogism

LAW OF SYLLOGISM

If the conclusion of one true conditional matches the hypothesis of another true conditional, you can chain them into one new conditional.

If a shape is a square, then it is a rectangle.
If a shape is a rectangle, then it is a quadrilateral.
Therefore: if a shape is a square, then it is a quadrilateral.

Telling Them Apart

Detachment pulls out a conclusion. Syllogism links two chains.

Law of Detachment: ONE conditional + its hypothesis being true → conclusion follows.
Law of Syllogism: TWO conditionals that chain together → a brand-new conditional follows.

💡 A quick tell: if you're given a specific fact ("this shape IS a square"), you're using Detachment. If you're only given two "if-then" rules with no specific fact, you're using Syllogism.

Worked Example

Choosing the right law, step by step

Problem: "If two angles are supplementary, their measures add to 180°." Angle A and Angle B are supplementary. What follows?

1
What are we given?
One conditional, plus a specific fact (A and B ARE supplementary).
2
Which law applies?
Law of Detachment — a specific fact matches the hypothesis.
3
Apply it
The measures of angle A and angle B add to 180°. This is guaranteed, not a guess.

Why This Matters

This is the engine that will run every proof from here on

Two-column proofs, which start in Lesson 2.6, are really just long chains built entirely from the Law of Detachment and Law of Syllogism applied over and over — each new line guaranteed by the postulates, definitions, or previous lines that came before it.

Real world: a detective using "if the suspect was in the building, the security camera caught them" plus "the camera caught them" concluding "the suspect was in the building" is using the Law of Detachment — the exact same logical structure, just in a different setting.
