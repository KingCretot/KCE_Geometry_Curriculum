Vocabulary

Three angle relationships you'll use constantly

1
Complementary Angles
Two angles whose measures add to 90°.
2
Supplementary Angles
Two angles whose measures add to 180°.
3
Vertical Angles
The pair of opposite angles formed when two lines cross. Vertical angles are always congruent — this is the Vertical Angles Theorem, and it's one of the most-used facts in this entire course.

Proving vertical angles are congruent

Angles 1 & 3 are vertical. Angles 2 & 4 are vertical.

Given: Lines cross, forming angles 1, 2, 3, 4 as shown. Angle 1 and Angle 2 form a straight line (supplementary). Angle 2 and Angle 3 form a straight line (supplementary).

Why This Matters

The Vertical Angles Theorem alone saves you a proof every time you use it

You just proved that vertical angles are congruent, from scratch, using only supplementary angle facts and substitution. That's the last time you'll ever need to prove it — from now on, "Vertical Angles Theorem" is a reason you can cite directly, exactly like a postulate.

Real world: surveyors and carpenters use vertical angles constantly — when two boards or beams cross, the angle on one side tells you the exact angle directly opposite it, without needing to measure it separately.
