Let's Begin — Final Lesson of Chapter 2!

Cutting something exactly, provably in half.

A "bisector" does exactly what it sounds like — it splits something into two exactly equal pieces. You've used bisectors informally your whole life (folding paper exactly in half). Today we make it formal, and prove it.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Define segment bisector and angle bisector
  • 2Use a bisector definition inside a two-column proof
  • 3Fill in a missing piece of a bisector proof

Vocabulary

Two kinds of bisectors

1
Segment Bisector
A point, line, or line segment that passes through the midpoint of a segment, splitting it into two congruent pieces.
2
Angle Bisector
A ray that splits an angle into two congruent angles, each exactly half the original.

Recall: a midpoint is the exact point where a segment bisector crosses — you already learned this term back in Lesson 2.6.

Worked Example

Proving with a segment bisector

Given: Point M bisects segment AB. AM = 3x. MB = x + 10.
Prove: x = 5
TWO-COLUMN PROOFStatement / Reason
StatementReason
M bisects segment AB. AM = 3x. MB = x + 10.Given
AM = MBDefinition of Segment Bisector
3x = x + 10Substitution Property
2x = 10Subtraction Property of Equality
x = 5Division Property of Equality

A Second Example

Proving with an angle bisector

Given: Ray BD bisects Angle ABC. Angle ABD = 34°.
Prove: Angle ABC = 68°
TWO-COLUMN PROOFStatement / Reason
StatementReason
Ray BD bisects Angle ABC. Angle ABD = 34°.Given
Angle ABD ≅ Angle DBCDefinition of Angle Bisector
Angle DBC = 34°Definition of Congruent Angles
Angle ABC = Angle ABD + Angle DBCAngle Addition Postulate
Angle ABC = 68°Substitution Property
