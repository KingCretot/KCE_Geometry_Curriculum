Let's Begin

Two different ways to measure a piece of a circle

An arc is a portion of a circle's circumference. Confusingly, arcs have two completely different measurements: arc measure (in degrees, matching the central angle) and arc length (an actual distance, in the same units as the radius). Today we learn both and the formula connecting them.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Distinguish minor arcs, major arcs, and semicircles
  • 2Find arc measure using the central angle
  • 3Calculate arc length using the arc-length formula

Core Vocabulary

Minor arcs, major arcs, and semicircles

A minor arc is smaller than a semicircle (less than 180°) and is named with two letters. A major arc is larger than a semicircle (more than 180°) and is named with three letters to avoid ambiguity. A semicircle is exactly half the circle (exactly 180°), cut off by a diameter.

Core Formula

Arc measure: the Central Angle Postulate

Arc Measure
measure of arc = measure of its central angle

An arc's measure in degrees is always identical to its central angle — the angle formed by the two radii to its endpoints. A major arc's measure is 360° minus its corresponding minor arc's measure.

Core Formula

Arc length

Arc Length
L = (θ/360) × 2πr

Arc length is an actual distance — it uses the same "fraction of the whole" idea as sector area from Lesson 7.6, but applied to the circumference (2πr) instead of the area. Don't confuse arc measure (degrees) with arc length (units of distance) — they answer different questions.

Worked Example

Finding both arc measure and arc length

Problem: A circle has radius 9. A central angle measures 60°. Find (a) the arc measure and (b) the arc length.

1
Arc measure
Equals the central angle: 60°
2
Arc length
L = (60/360) × 2π(9) = ⅙ × 18π = 3π ≈ 9.42 units

Why This Matters

Running tracks and clock hands

Every lane of a running track is a slightly different arc length around the curved ends — that's why outer lanes get a staggered starting position, calculated directly from this arc-length formula.

Real world: the distance a clock's minute hand tip travels in 15 minutes is exactly an arc length calculation — a 90° arc on a circle whose radius equals the hand's length.
