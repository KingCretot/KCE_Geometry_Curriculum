Key Ideas · Lessons 8.1–8.2

Chords and tangents

Lesson 8.1

Chords

A chord joins two points on a circle; the diameter is the longest chord. A radius perpendicular to a chord bisects it. Chords the same distance from the center are congruent, and chords closer to the center are longer.

Example: Radius 10, chord 6 units from center: half-chord = √(100 − 36) = 8, chord = 16.
Lesson 8.2

Tangents

A tangent touches the circle at exactly one point and is perpendicular to the radius there. Two tangent segments from the same outside point are congruent.

Example: Radius 5, outside point 13 from center: tangent length = √(169 − 25) = 12.
Tangent ⊥ radius at the point of tangency

Key Ideas · Lessons 8.3–8.5

Arcs and angles

Lesson 8.3

Arc measure vs. arc length

An arc's measure (degrees) equals its central angle. Minor arcs are under 180°; a major arc is 360° minus its minor arc. Arc length is a distance: L = (θ/360)·2πr.

Example: Radius 9, 80° arc: L = (80/360)(18π) = 4π.
Lesson 8.4

Inscribed angles

An inscribed angle has its vertex on the circle and equals half its intercepted arc. Inscribed angles on the same arc are congruent. An angle inscribed in a semicircle is 90°.

Example: An inscribed angle on a 100° arc measures 50°.
Inscribed angle = ½ × intercepted arc
Lesson 8.5

Angles inside and outside

Two chords crossing inside: angle = ½(sum of the intercepted arcs). Secants or tangents meeting outside: angle = ½(far arc − near arc).

Example: Inside, arcs 80° and 40°: 60°. Outside, arcs 140° and 40°: 50°.

Key Ideas · Lessons 8.6–8.7

Constructions and equations

Lesson 8.6

Circles around and inside triangles

The circumscribed circle passes through all three vertices; its center is the circumcenter (perpendicular bisectors). The inscribed circle touches all three sides; its center is the incenter (angle bisectors).

Example: A tangent from an outside point is constructed using the tangent ⊥ radius fact.
Lesson 8.7

Equation of a circle

(x − h)² + (y − k)² = r² has center (h, k) and radius r. Plug in a point: less than r² is inside, equal is on, greater is outside.

Example: (x − 2)² + (y + 5)² = 36: center (2, −5), radius 6.

Watch Out

The mistakes that cost the most points

The mistakeThe fix
Treating arc length and arc measure as the sameMeasure is in degrees (the central angle). Length is a distance in units, (θ/360)·2πr.
Using the full arc for an inscribed angleInscribed angle = half the arc. The central angle is the one that equals the arc.
Mixing up inside and outside angle formulasInside (chords): half the SUM. Outside (secants/tangents): half the DIFFERENCE.
Forgetting to double the half-chordThe perpendicular from the center gives half the chord. Double it for the full length.
Sign errors in the circle equation(x + 3)² means h = −3. The center's signs are opposite the signs in the equation.
Using r instead of r² on the right sideRadius 5 → the equation ends in = 25.

Practice Test

10 questions, every lesson mixed together

Just like a unit test, these jump between lessons. You get two tries on each question, and every question explains itself after you answer.

Question 1
A circle has radius 10. A chord is 6 units from the center. How long is the chord?
Here's why: The perpendicular from the center bisects the chord, making a right triangle: half-chord = √(10² − 6²) = √64 = 8. The full chord is 2 × 8 = 16.
Question 2
A circle has radius 5. Point P is 13 units from the center. How long is a tangent segment from P?
Here's why: The tangent is perpendicular to the radius, so 5² + t² = 13²: t² = 169 − 25 = 144, t = 12. 8 subtracts the lengths without squaring.
Question 3
PA and PB are tangent segments from the same point P. PA = 3x + 2 and PB = 5x − 6. What is PA?
Here's why: Tangents from one point are congruent: 3x + 2 = 5x − 6, so x = 4 and PA = 3(4) + 2 = 14.
Question 4
A circle has radius 9. What is the length of an 80° arc?
Here's why: L = (80/360)(2π · 9) = (2/9)(18π) = 4π, about 12.6 units. 8π comes from putting the diameter (18) in place of the radius.
Question 5
A minor arc measures 130°. What does its major arc measure?
Here's why: The minor and major arcs make the whole circle: 360° − 130° = 230°. 50° subtracts from 180° instead.
Question 6
An inscribed angle measures 35°. What is the measure of its intercepted arc?
Here's why: An inscribed angle is half its arc, so the arc is double: 2 × 35° = 70°.
Question 7
A triangle is inscribed in a circle with one side as a diameter. One of the other angles is 28°. What is the third angle?
Here's why: The angle opposite the diameter is inscribed in a semicircle, so it's 90°. The third angle is 180° − 90° − 28° = 62°.
Question 8
Two chords intersect inside a circle. The intercepted arcs measure 80° and 40°. What is the angle?
Here's why: Inside the circle, use half the sum: ½(80° + 40°) = 60°. 20° uses the outside (difference) formula.
Question 9
Two secants meet outside a circle. The far arc is 140° and the near arc is 40°. What is the angle?
Here's why: Outside the circle, use half the difference: ½(140° − 40°) = 50°. 90° uses the sum formula, and 100° forgets the ½.
Question 10
Which is the equation of the circle with center (−3, 4) and radius 5?
Here's why: (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r² with h = −3, k = 4, r = 5: x − (−3) = x + 3, and r² = 25.
