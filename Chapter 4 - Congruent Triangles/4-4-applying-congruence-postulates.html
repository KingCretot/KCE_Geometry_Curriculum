A Bonus Shortcut

AAS — Angle-Angle-Side

AAS Theorem: if two angles and a non-included side of one triangle are congruent to two angles and the corresponding non-included side of another triangle, the triangles are congruent.

Why this works: if you know two angles of a triangle, the Triangle Angle-Sum Theorem from Lesson 4.1 tells you the third angle too — so AAS is really just ASA in disguise, since now you effectively know two angles and the side between them.

The First Trap

Why AAA does NOT prove congruence

DOES NOT WORK

Two triangles can have all three pairs of angles congruent while being completely different sizes — one just a scaled-up copy of the other. Matching angles only guarantees the same shape, not the same size.

SAME ANGLES, DIFFERENT SIZES

Same three angle measures — but clearly not the same triangle.

The Second Trap

Why SSA does NOT prove congruence

DOES NOT WORK

Two sides plus a non-included angle can sometimes swing into two genuinely different triangles — the third side has two possible valid lengths that both satisfy the given information. Because the result isn't always unique, SSA is never accepted as a congruence shortcut.

Memory trick: SSA spelled backward is a word you don't say in geometry class — a useful (if silly) reminder that it doesn't work.

Why This Matters

Knowing which shortcuts to trust

An engineer double-checking a supplier's parts needs to know exactly which measurements guarantee an exact match versus which ones might allow a subtly wrong part through. The same discipline that tells you AAA only proves similar shape, not identical size, protects against costly manufacturing mistakes.

Real world: a photo enlarged to a poster keeps all its angles (AAA) but is a completely different size than the original — exactly why AAA alone never proves two things are truly identical.
