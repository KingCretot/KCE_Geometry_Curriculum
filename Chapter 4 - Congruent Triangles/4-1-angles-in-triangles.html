Let's Begin — Welcome to Chapter 4!

Every triangle keeps the same secret

Take any triangle you can imagine — tall and skinny, short and wide, tilted sideways — and its three interior angles will always add up to the exact same number. Today we prove why, and use that fact to find missing angles without measuring a single one.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1State and apply the Triangle Angle-Sum Theorem
  • 2Find a missing interior angle of a triangle
  • 3Use the Exterior Angle Theorem to find angle measures

The Big Theorem

The Triangle Angle-Sum Theorem

In Any Triangle
m∠A + m∠B + m∠C = 180°
ALWAYS TRUE, EVERY TRIANGLE

No matter the triangle's shape, these three angles always sum to 180°.

Worked Example

Finding a missing angle

Problem: Triangle DEF has ∠D = 52° and ∠E = 65°. Find ∠F.

1
Set up the equation
52° + 65° + m∠F = 180°
2
Combine known angles
117° + m∠F = 180°
3
Solve
m∠F = 63°

A Second Theorem

The Exterior Angle Theorem

EXTEND A SIDE OUTWARD

Exterior ∠ACD is formed by extending side BC outward past C.

Exterior Angle Theorem
m∠ACD = m∠A + m∠B

An exterior angle of a triangle always equals the sum of the two non-adjacent interior angles — the two angles that aren't right next to it.

Worked Example

Using the Exterior Angle Theorem

Problem: In triangle ABC, ∠A = 48° and ∠B = 71°. Find the exterior angle at C.

1
Apply the theorem directly
Exterior ∠C = m∠A + m∠B
2
Substitute
Exterior ∠C = 48° + 71° = 119°
3
Check with the Angle-Sum Theorem
Interior ∠C = 180° − 119° = 61°, and 48° + 71° + 61° = 180° ✓

Why This Matters

A rule so reliable, entire trades depend on it

A roofer cutting rafters, a carpenter framing a gable, or a surveyor triangulating land all rely on the fact that a triangle's angles are locked to exactly 180° — measure any two, and the third is guaranteed, no tape measure needed for that last cut.

Real world: sailors and pilots have used triangle angle relationships for centuries to triangulate position from two known landmarks — the same 180° guarantee from this lesson, applied at sea.
