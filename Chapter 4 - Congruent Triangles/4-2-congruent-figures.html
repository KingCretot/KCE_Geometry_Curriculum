Let's Begin

Same shape, same size, same everything

"Congruent" means two figures are identical in every way — same angles, same side lengths, just possibly in a different position or orientation. Today we make that precise for triangles, including a strict rule about the order you name their vertices.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Define congruent triangles using corresponding parts
  • 2Name congruent triangles in the correct correspondence order
  • 3Use CPCTC to identify congruent corresponding parts

Definition

What "congruent triangles" really means

TRIANGLE ABC ≅ TRIANGLE DEF

Same size, same shape — picked up and moved, they'd fit exactly on top of each other.

Two triangles are congruent when all three pairs of corresponding sides AND all three pairs of corresponding angles are congruent — six total matches.

The Order Matters

Naming congruent triangles correctly

When you write ΔABC ≅ ΔDEF, you're not just saying the two triangles match — you're saying exactly which vertex matches which. A comes before B and C, so A must correspond to D, the first letter on the other side.

CorrespondenceMeaning
A ↔ D∠A ≅ ∠D
B ↔ E∠B ≅ ∠E
C ↔ F∠C ≅ ∠F
AB ↔ DEside AB ≅ side DE
BC ↔ EFside BC ≅ side EF
AC ↔ DFside AC ≅ side DF

Watch out: writing ΔABC ≅ ΔEFD would claim A ↔ E, not A ↔ D — a completely different (and likely wrong) correspondence, even though it's the "same" two triangles.

A Powerful Shortcut

CPCTC: Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Congruent

The idea: once you've proven two triangles are congruent by any method, CPCTC lets you immediately claim any pair of their corresponding parts is congruent too — without proving each one separately.

1
Step 1
Prove ΔABC ≅ ΔDEF (using a method from upcoming lessons)
2
Step 2
Once congruence is established, CPCTC lets you state, for example, ∠B ≅ ∠E automatically

Reading a correspondence statement

1

2
Side ST
S↔Y and T↔Z, so ST corresponds to YZ
3
Angle T
T↔Z, so ∠T corresponds to ∠Z

Why This Matters

Interchangeable parts, built on congruence

Manufacturing depends entirely on congruent shapes: every bolt of a given size fits every matching nut because they were made congruent to spec. An architect designing identical support trusses for a roofline relies on the same guarantee — make one correctly, and every congruent copy performs identically.

Real world: stamped sheet-metal parts for cars, identical replacement tiles for a floor, matched picture frames — all rely on congruence, the same six-part match this lesson defines.
