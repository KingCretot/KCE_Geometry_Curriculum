Let's Begin

How much does it hold?

Surface area covered the outside of a solid. Volume measures the inside: how much space the solid fills, or how much it can hold. Today’s formula, V = Bh, works for every prism and every cylinder.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Define volume and give its units
  • 2Calculate the volume of a prism or cylinder
  • 3Use Cavalieri’s Principle and find a missing dimension from a known volume

Vocabulary

What volume means

The volume of a solid is the number of unit cubes it takes to fill it. A unit cube is 1 unit on each edge, so volume is measured in cubic units: cubic inches (in³), cubic feet (ft³), cubic centimeters (cm³).

Area is square units because it has two dimensions. Volume is cubic units because it has three. A box 4 × 3 × 2 holds 4 × 3 = 12 cubes in one layer, and 2 layers make 24 cubes.

Core Formula

Volume of a prism

Prism
V = Bh

B is the area of one base and h is the height. That is the “layers” idea: B unit cubes fit in each layer one unit thick, and there are h layers. For a rectangular prism, B = lw, so V = lwh.

Core Formula

Volume of a cylinder

Cylinder
V = Bh = πr²h
RADIUS r, HEIGHT h

Same idea, same formula: the base is a circle with area πr², stacked h units high.

Cavalieri's Principle

Leaning doesn’t change the volume

Cavalieri’s Principle: if two solids have the same height and every slice at the same level has the same area, the two solids have the same volume.

Picture a neat stack of 20 coins, then push it so it leans to one side. The stack is now an oblique cylinder, but it is the same 20 coins, so the volume has not changed. That is why V = Bh works for oblique prisms and cylinders too, as long as h is the perpendicular height, not the slanted length.

Worked Example

Three volume problems

Problem: (a) A triangular prism has a right-triangle base with legs 6 and 8 and height 10. (b) A cylinder has radius 4 and height 9. (c) A rectangular prism has volume 120, length 5, and width 4. Find its height.

1
Triangular prism
B = ½(6)(8) = 24, so V = 24(10) = 240 cubic units
2
Cylinder
V = π(4)²(9) = 144π ≈ 452.4 cubic units
3
Missing height
120 = 5(4)h, so 120 = 20h and h = 6 units

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Find each volume.

Question 1 — A rectangular prism measures 3 by 4 by 5. What is its volume?

Here's why: V = lwh = 3 × 4 × 5 = 60. 94 is the surface area, the cover rather than the contents.

Question 2 — A cylinder has radius 3 and height 10. What is its volume?

Here's why: V = πr²h = π(9)(10) = 90π. 60π is the lateral area, 2πrh, and 30π forgets to square the radius.

Why This Matters

Pools, concrete, and shipping containers

A pool company fills a rectangular pool by computing lwh in cubic feet and converting to gallons. A contractor ordering concrete for a round column computes πr²h, because concrete is sold by the cubic yard.

Real world: a standard 20-foot shipping container is a rectangular prism, and freight companies price space inside it by volume.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
What is the volume of a cube with edges of length 5?
Here's why: V = 5 × 5 × 5 = 125. 25 is one face, and 150 is the surface area.
Question 2
A cylinder has radius 2 and height 7. What is its volume?
Here's why: V = π(2)²(7) = 28π. 14π forgets to square the radius.
Question 3
A right cylinder and an oblique cylinder both have radius 3 and height 10. How do their volumes compare?
Here's why: By Cavalieri’s Principle, same height and same cross section at every level means same volume. Both are 90π.
Question 4
A prism has volume 200 and base area 25. What is its height?
Here's why: V = Bh, so 200 = 25h and h = 8. 175 subtracts instead of dividing.
Question 5 — True or False
Volume is measured in square units.
Here's why: False. Volume has three dimensions, so it is measured in cubic units. Square units are for area.
