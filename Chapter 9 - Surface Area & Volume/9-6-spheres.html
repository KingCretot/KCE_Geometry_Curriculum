Lesson 9.6 · Surface Area & Volume of Spheres

Let's Begin

The roundest solid there is

A sphere has no faces, no edges, and no base, so none of our formulas so far apply. It gets two of its own, and they depend only on the radius.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Define sphere, great circle, and hemisphere
  • 2Calculate the surface area and volume of a sphere or hemisphere
  • 3Find a sphere’s radius from its surface area or volume

Vocabulary

Parts of a sphere

CENTER, RADIUS, AND A GREAT CIRCLE

A sphere is the set of all points in space that are the same distance, the radius, from a center point. A great circle is where a plane through the center slices the sphere, the biggest circle you can draw on it. A great circle cuts the sphere into two hemispheres.

Core Formula

Surface area of a sphere

Sphere Surface Area
S = 4πr²

A sphere’s surface is exactly four times the area of one of its great circles (πr²). Peel an orange, and the peel will cover about four circles traced around the orange’s middle.

Core Formula

Volume of a sphere

Sphere Volume
V = &frac43;πr³

The radius is cubed because volume has three dimensions. Double the radius of a ball and it holds 2³ = 8 times as much air.

Worked Example

A sphere and a hemisphere

Problem: A sphere has radius 6. Find (a) its surface area, (b) its volume, and (c) the volume and total surface area of half of it.

1
Surface area
S = 4π(6)² = 144π ≈ 452.4 square units
2
Volume
V = &frac43;π(6)³ = &frac43;π(216) = 288π ≈ 904.8 cubic units
3
Hemisphere volume
Half the sphere: 144π ≈ 452.4 cubic units
4
Hemisphere surface area
Half the curved surface, 2πr² = 72π, plus the flat great-circle base, πr² = 36π, for 108π ≈ 339.3 square units

Worked Example

Working backward to the radius

Problem: (a) A sphere has surface area 100π. (b) Another has volume 36π. Find each radius.

1
From surface area
4πr² = 100π, so r² = 25 and r = 5
2
From volume
&frac43;πr³ = 36π, so r³ = 36 × ¾ = 27 and r = 3

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

A sphere has radius 3.

Question 1 — What is its surface area?

Here's why: S = 4π(3)² = 36π. 9π is the area of one great circle, a quarter of the surface.

Question 2 — What is its volume?

Here's why: V = &frac43;π(27) = 36π. It is a coincidence that the surface area and volume share the number 36 when r = 3; their units are different. 108π forgets to divide by 3.

Why This Matters

Balls, bubbles, and planets

A sphere holds the most volume for the least surface area of any shape. That is why soap bubbles are round, why water drops pull into spheres, and why many water towers and propane tanks are spherical: less metal for the same amount stored.

Real world: Earth is very nearly a sphere with radius about 3,960 miles, so its surface area is about 4π(3,960)² ≈ 197 million square miles.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
What is the surface area of a sphere with radius 5?
Here's why: S = 4π(25) = 100π. 25π is one great circle, and (500/3)π is the volume.
Question 2
What is the volume of a sphere with radius 3?
Here's why: V = &frac43;π(27) = 36π. 27π leaves out the &frac43;.
Question 3
A great circle of a sphere is...
Here's why: Any plane through the center makes a great circle, and it has the same radius as the sphere. The equator is one great circle, but there are infinitely many.
Question 4
A sphere has surface area 64π. What is its radius?
Here's why: 4πr² = 64π, so r² = 16 and r = 4. 16 is r², not r.
Question 5 — True or False
The total surface area of a hemisphere, including its flat base, is 2πr².
Here's why: False. The curved part is 2πr², but the flat circle base adds πr², for 3πr² total.
