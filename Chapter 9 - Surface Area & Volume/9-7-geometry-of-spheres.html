Let's Begin

What happens to geometry on a ball?

Everything since Chapter 1 has lived on a flat plane. But we live on a sphere. Today we locate points on a globe, find distances along its surface, and discover that on a sphere some of geometry’s most basic rules stop working.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Locate points on Earth using latitude and longitude
  • 2Find the arc length between two points on the same great circle
  • 3Contrast spherical geometry with the flat (Euclidean) geometry we’ve studied

Earth’s Grid

Latitude and longitude

THE EQUATOR, A PARALLEL, AND MERIDIANS

Red: the equator. Blue: the 30°N parallel. Gold: meridians, running pole to pole.

Latitude measures how far north or south of the equator a point is, from 0° at the equator to 90° at each pole. Circles of equal latitude are called parallels. Longitude measures east or west of the Prime Meridian in Greenwich, England, from 0° to 180°. Half-circles of equal longitude are meridians.

The equator and every full meridian circle are great circles. The other parallels are smaller circles, not great circles.

Spherical Geometry

What counts as a “line” on a sphere

On a flat plane, the shortest path between two points is a straight segment. On a sphere, you can’t travel in a straight line without leaving the surface, so the shortest path is an arc of a great circle.

In spherical geometry, a “line” is a great circle. Points are still points, but every pair of “lines” crosses, at two points on opposite sides of the sphere.

Core Formula

Distance along a great circle

Great-Circle Arc Length
L = (θ/360) × 2πr

This is the arc-length formula from Lesson 8.3, applied to a great circle. θ is the central angle between the two points, measured at the center of the sphere.

For two places on the same meridian, θ is the difference in their latitudes if both are in the same hemisphere, and the sum if one is north and the other is south. Earth’s radius is about 3,960 miles, so its circumference is about 2π(3,960) ≈ 24,881 miles.

Two Geometries

Flat versus spherical

IdeaEuclidean (flat)Spherical
A lineStraight, infiniteA great circle, finite
Parallel lines through a point not on a lineExactly oneNone: every two great circles meet
Two lines intersect inAt most one pointExactly two points
Triangle angle sumExactly 180°More than 180°

Try it: start at the North Pole, walk straight down a meridian to the equator, turn 90° and walk a quarter of the way around the equator, then turn 90° and walk back to the pole. Your triangle has three right angles, a sum of 270°.

Worked Example

Distance between two cities on one meridian

Problem: City A is at 40°N and City B is at 10°S, both on the same meridian. Using Earth’s radius of 3,960 miles, how far apart are they along the surface?

1
Find the central angle
One city is north and one is south, so add: 40° + 10° = 50°.
2
Apply the formula
L = (50/360) × 2π(3,960) ≈ (50/360) × 24,881
3
Compute
L ≈ 3,456 miles

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Think about great circles on a globe.

Question 1 — Which of these is a great circle?

Here's why: A great circle’s plane passes through Earth’s center. The equator does; every other parallel sits above or below the center, so it is a smaller circle.

Question 2 — On a sphere with radius 10, two points on a great circle are 90° apart. How long is the arc between them?

Here's why: L = (90/360) × 2π(10) = ¼ × 20π = 5π. 20π is the whole great circle, and 10π is half of it.

Why This Matters

Why flights to Asia fly over Alaska

On a flat map, a flight from Chicago to Tokyo looks like it should head straight west. But the shortest route is a great-circle arc, which curves far north over Canada and Alaska. Airlines fly great-circle routes because they save fuel and time.

Real world: GPS receivers, ship navigators, and air traffic systems all compute distances with great-circle arcs, not flat straight lines.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
In spherical geometry, a “line” is...
Here's why: The shortest path on a sphere follows a great circle, so great circles play the role of lines. Among the parallels, only the equator is a great circle.
Question 2
In spherical geometry, how many lines through a point are parallel to a given line?
Here's why: Any two great circles cross each other, so no two lines on a sphere are parallel. Exactly one is the rule on a flat plane.
Question 3
The angles of a triangle drawn on a sphere add up to...
Here's why: The surface curves outward, so the angles get bigger. The pole-to-equator triangle has three 90° angles, 270° total.
Question 4
On a globe with radius 6, two cities on the same meridian are 60° apart. How long is the arc between them?
Here's why: L = (60/360) × 2π(6) = ⅙ × 12π = 2π. 12π is the whole great circle.
Question 5 — True or False
Every line of latitude is a great circle.
Here's why: False. Only the equator is. The other parallels get smaller as they approach the poles.
