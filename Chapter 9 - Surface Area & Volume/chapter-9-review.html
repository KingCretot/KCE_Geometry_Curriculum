Key Ideas · Lesson 9.1

Reading a solid

Lesson 9.1

Euler, cross sections, and solids of rotation

Polyhedra (flat faces only) obey V − E + F = 2. Cylinders, cones, and spheres have curved surfaces, so they aren't polyhedra. Slicing a solid gives a cross section; spinning a flat shape around a line makes a solid.

Example: Rotating a right triangle around a leg makes a cone. Slicing a cube parallel to a face gives a square.

Key Ideas · Lessons 9.2–9.3

Surface area

Lesson 9.2

Prisms and cylinders

Lateral area is the sides only; surface area adds both bases. Prism: L = Ph, S = Ph + 2B. Cylinder: L = 2πrh, S = 2πrh + 2πr².

Example: Cylinder r = 3, h = 7: S = 42π + 18π = 60π.

Lesson 9.3

Pyramids and cones use slant height

Slant height l runs along a face; height h runs straight down the middle. They form a right triangle with the radius (or half the base edge): r² + h² = l². Pyramid: S = ½Pl + B. Cone: S = πrl + πr².

Example: Cone r = 6, h = 8: l = 10, S = 60π + 36π = 96π.

Key Ideas · Lessons 9.4–9.6

Volume

Lesson 9.4

Prisms and cylinders: V = Bh

Volume is base area times height, in cubic units. Cylinder: V = πr²h. Cavalieri's Principle: solids with the same height and same cross-sectional areas have the same volume, so slanted (oblique) versions use the same formula.

Example: Cylinder r = 2, h = 7: V = 28π.
Lesson 9.5

Pyramids and cones: one-third

A pyramid or cone holds exactly one-third of the matching prism or cylinder: V = ⅓Bh, cone V = ⅓πr²h. Use h, not the slant height.

Example: Cone r = 6, h = 5: ⅓(36π)(5) = 60π.
Lesson 9.6

Spheres

S = 4πr² and V = (4/3)πr³. A great circle is a slice through the center. A hemisphere's total surface is 2πr² curved + πr² flat = 3πr².

Example: r = 6: S = 144π, V = 288π.

Key Ideas · Lessons 9.7–9.8

Spheres and drawings

Lesson 9.7

Geometry on a sphere

On a sphere, "lines" are great circles. There are no parallel lines, and a triangle's angles add to more than 180°. Distance along a great circle is an arc length: L = (θ/360)·2πr.

Example: Globe radius 12, points 90° apart: L = ¼(24π) = 6π.
Lesson 9.8

Orthographic, isometric, perspective

Orthographic: separate flat top, front, and side views. Isometric: edges drawn 120° apart, parallel edges stay parallel. Perspective: edges recede to one or two vanishing points.

Example: An upright cylinder: top view is a circle, front view a rectangle.

Watch Out

The mistakes that cost the most points

The mistakeThe fix
Using slant height for volumeVolume uses the true height h. Slant height l is only for lateral and surface area.
Using height for a cone's lateral areaπrl needs the slant height. Find it with r² + h² = l² first.
Forgetting the ⅓ for pyramids and conesV = ⅓Bh. Without it you get the prism or cylinder.
Counting one base instead of twoPrism and cylinder surface area add 2B. Pyramids and cones have just one base.
Mixing square and cubic unitsArea and surface area: square units. Volume: cubic units.
Mixing up the sphere formulasS = 4πr² (squared, area). V = (4/3)πr³ (cubed, volume).

Practice Test

10 questions, every lesson mixed together

Just like a unit test, these jump between lessons. You get two tries on each question, and every question explains itself after you answer.

Question 1
A hexagonal prism has 12 vertices and 8 faces. How many edges does it have?
Here's why: V − E + F = 2 → 12 − E + 8 = 2 → E = 18. Check: 6 edges on each hexagon plus 6 connecting edges = 18.
Question 2
What is the surface area of a 5 × 4 × 3 rectangular prism?
Here's why: S = 2(5·4 + 5·3 + 4·3) = 2(20 + 15 + 12) = 94. 60 is the volume, 47 forgets to double, and 54 is the lateral area (Ph with height 3).
Question 3
A cylinder has radius 3 and height 7. What is its surface area?
Here's why: Lateral 2π(3)(7) = 42π, plus two bases 2π(3²) = 18π, total 60π. 42π is lateral only, 51π adds one base, and 63π is the volume.
Question 4
A cone has radius 6 and height 8. What is its surface area?
Here's why: First the slant height: √(36 + 64) = 10. S = πrl + πr² = 60π + 36π = 96π. 84π uses the height (8) in place of the slant height.
Question 5
A regular square pyramid has base edge 10 and slant height 13. What is its surface area?
Here's why: P = 40, so lateral area = ½(40)(13) = 260. Add the base, 10² = 100: S = 360. 620 forgets the ½.
Question 6
A cylinder stands upright on one of its bases. What is its top view in an orthographic drawing?
Here's why: Looking straight down, you see only the circular top. The front and side views of an upright cylinder are rectangles.
Question 7
What is the volume of a cone with radius 6 and height 5?
Here's why: V = ⅓πr²h = ⅓(36π)(5) = 60π. 180π is the cylinder (no ⅓).
Question 8
A cylinder has volume 72π and radius 3. What is its height?
Here's why: V = πr²h → 72π = 9πh → h = 8. 24 divides by r instead of r², and 12 divides by 2r (the diameter).
Question 9
What is the volume of a sphere with radius 6?
Here's why: V = (4/3)π(6³) = (4/3)(216)π = 288π. 144π is the surface area, and 864π leaves out the division by 3.
Question 10
On a globe with radius 12, two points lie on the same great circle, 90° apart. How long is the arc between them?
Here's why: L = (90/360)(2π · 12) = ¼(24π) = 6π. 24π is the whole great circle.
