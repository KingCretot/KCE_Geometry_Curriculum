Let's Begin

Last week we assumed the lines were parallel. Today we prove it.

Lesson 3.1 started with "if l ∥ m, then..." But how does anyone know two lines are parallel in the first place, before they've been carefully drawn with a ruler? By flipping every postulate around — using the angles to prove the lines are parallel.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1State the converse of the Corresponding Angles Postulate
  • 2Use congruent alternate interior, alternate exterior, or corresponding angles to prove two lines are parallel
  • 3Use supplementary consecutive interior angles to prove two lines are parallel

A Key Idea

The converse: flip the statement around

Converse means swapping the "if" and "then" parts of a conditional statement. Lesson 3.1's postulate said: if lines are parallel, then corresponding angles are congruent. The converse says: if corresponding angles are congruent, then the lines are parallel.

l ∥ m
→
∠1 ≅ ∠5
∠1 ≅ ∠5
→
l ∥ m

Both directions are true here — that's what makes this set of converse theorems so useful for proofs: we can travel from angle facts to line facts, or line facts to angle facts, whichever direction a problem needs.

Four Ways to Prove Parallel

Any one of these is enough

CONVERSE THEOREMS
  • Converse of Corresponding Angles: if corresponding angles are congruent, the lines are parallel.
  • Converse of Alternate Interior Angles: if alternate interior angles are congruent, the lines are parallel.
  • Converse of Alternate Exterior Angles: if alternate exterior angles are congruent, the lines are parallel.
  • Converse of Consecutive Interior Angles: if consecutive interior angles are supplementary, the lines are parallel.

Worked Example

Choosing the right converse theorem

Problem: A transversal crosses lines p and q. ∠4 (interior, on p) and ∠5 (interior, on q, opposite side of the transversal from ∠4) both measure 73°. Prove p ∥ q.

1
Identify the angle pair
∠4 and ∠5 are interior angles on opposite sides of the transversal — that makes them alternate interior angles.
2
Check the relationship
∠4 = 73° and ∠5 = 73°, so ∠4 ≅ ∠5.
3
Apply the converse theorem
By the Converse of Alternate Interior Angles, since ∠4 ≅ ∠5, p ∥ q.

Why This Matters

Inspectors don't just measure — they prove

A building inspector checking that a wall is truly plumb and parallel to the wall across from it doesn't just eyeball it — they measure specific angles against a known transversal (like a laser level line) and use exactly these converse relationships to confirm the walls are parallel before signing off.

Real world: when installing kitchen cabinets, a carpenter checks that the top edges of two cabinets are parallel by measuring the angle each makes against a single straight reference line (a transversal) — matching angles confirm the cabinets line up.
