Let's Begin — Welcome to Chapter 3!

Two lines that never meet, and the line that crosses both

Railroad tracks. The lines of a notebook page. Opposite edges of a doorframe. Parallel lines are everywhere — and the moment a third line crosses them, geometry hands us eight brand-new angles, all related to each other in predictable ways.

Chapter 2 was about how to reason and prove. Chapter 3 gives us our first real geometric object to reason about: parallel lines cut by a transversal.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Identify parallel, perpendicular, and skew lines
  • 2Identify a transversal and the eight angles it creates
  • 3Apply the Corresponding Angles Postulate when two lines are parallel

Vocabulary

The players in every parallel-lines diagram

1
Parallel Lines
Coplanar lines that never intersect, no matter how far they extend. We write l ∥ m and mark them with matching arrowheads in a diagram.
2
Transversal
A line that intersects two or more coplanar lines at two distinct points. The transversal is what creates all the angle relationships we'll study today.
3
Corresponding Angles
A pair of angles in the same relative position at each intersection — for example, both in the upper-left. When the two lines are parallel, corresponding angles are congruent.

Also worth knowing: two lines in the same plane that are not parallel must intersect. Two lines that don't intersect and aren't parallel — because they're not even in the same plane — are called skew lines. Perpendicular lines are a special case: they intersect at a 90° angle, written l ⊥ m.

Seeing It in Action

Eight angles, one transversal

CORRESPONDING ANGLES POSTULATE

If l ∥ m, then ∠1 ≅ ∠5, ∠2 ≅ ∠6, ∠3 ≅ ∠7, and ∠4 ≅ ∠8 — each pair sits in the exact same corner at its own intersection.

Three More Relationships

Not every pair is congruent — some are supplementary

Using the numbering from the last slide (angles 3, 4, 5, 6 sit between the two parallel lines — the "interior" angles; 1, 2, 7, 8 sit outside them):

Alternate Interior
Congruent
∠4 & ∠6, and ∠3 & ∠5 — interior, opposite sides of the transversal
Alternate Exterior
Congruent
∠1 & ∠7, and ∠2 & ∠8 — exterior, opposite sides of the transversal
Consecutive Interior
Supplementary (sum to 180°)
∠3 & ∠6, and ∠4 & ∠5 — interior, same side of the transversal. Also called co-interior or same-side interior angles.

Worked Example

One given angle unlocks all eight

Problem: l ∥ m, and a transversal crosses both. ∠3 = 65°. Find ∠7 (corresponding), ∠5 (alternate interior), and ∠6 (consecutive interior partner of ∠3).

1
∠7 — corresponding to ∠3
Corresponding angles are congruent when lines are parallel, so ∠7 = 65°.
2
∠5 — alternate interior with ∠3
Alternate interior angles are congruent, so ∠5 = 65°.
3
∠6 — consecutive interior with ∠3
Consecutive interior angles are supplementary: ∠3 + ∠6 = 180°, so ∠6 = 180° − 65° = 115°.

Why This Matters

Every straight staircase and every set of railroad tracks depends on this

Builders check that walls are parallel and that a stairway's stringers stay parallel to each other using exactly these angle relationships — if a transversal crossing two boards doesn't produce congruent corresponding angles, the boards aren't truly parallel, and the structure will be off.

Real world: road crews painting parallel lane lines use a transversal-style guide across the road to check both painted lines are cut at matching angles — if the corresponding angles aren't equal, the lines are drifting apart or together instead of staying parallel.
