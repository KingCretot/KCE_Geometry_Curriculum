Let's Begin

From proving lines are parallel to building them yourself

Lessons 3.1 and 3.2 were about lines someone else already drew. Today you become the one holding the compass and straightedge — constructing a parallel line, a perpendicular line, and a perpendicular bisector using only those two tools, no protractor allowed.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Construct a line parallel to a given line through a point not on the line
  • 2Construct a line perpendicular to a given line through a point on or off the line
  • 3Construct the perpendicular bisector of a segment

The Tools

Compass and straightedge — nothing else

1
Straightedge
Draws a straight line between two points. It has no markings — you cannot use it to measure a distance, only to connect points you've already located.
2
Compass
Draws a circle or arc at a fixed radius from a center point. This is how every distance in a construction gets copied exactly, without ever writing down a number.

Why this matters: a construction proves something is exactly true — not just approximately true from careful drawing. Every construction in this lesson relies on one repeated idea: where two arcs of equal radius intersect, you've found a point that's exactly equidistant from both centers.

Construction 1

Perpendicular bisector of a segment

STEP-BY-STEP CONSTRUCTION
  1. 1Open the compass wider than half of segment AB. Center it on A and draw a full arc.
  2. 2Keep the same compass width. Center it on B and draw another full arc, crossing the first arc at two points.
  3. 3Use the straightedge to connect those two intersection points. That line is the perpendicular bisector — it crosses AB at its exact midpoint, M, at exactly 90°.

Construction 2

A perpendicular through a point on the line

STEP-BY-STEP CONSTRUCTION
  1. 1With the compass centered on point P (on the line), mark two points on the line equidistant from P — one on each side.
  2. 2Widen the compass. From each of those two points, draw arcs above the line that intersect each other.
  3. 3Connect that intersection point to P with the straightedge — that new line is perpendicular to the original at exactly P.

Construction 3

A parallel line through a point not on the line

STEP-BY-STEP CONSTRUCTION
  1. 1Draw a transversal line through point P that also crosses the original line, creating an angle at the crossing point.
  2. 2Copy that exact angle at point P, on the same side of the transversal, using compass arcs to duplicate the angle without measuring it.
  3. 3The new ray from P — forming a congruent corresponding angle — is parallel to the original line, by the Converse of the Corresponding Angles Postulate from Lesson 3.2.

Why This Matters

No tape measure, no problem — and no rounding error

Long before laser levels existed, builders and surveyors used exactly these compass-and-straightedge methods to lay out foundations with true right angles and truly parallel walls — a measured angle can drift by a fraction of a degree, but a constructed one is exact by the geometry itself.

Real world: a landscaper laying out a rectangular garden bed without a laser tool can still guarantee square corners by constructing perpendiculars this same way — stake, string, and arcs instead of compass and paper, same underlying geometry.
