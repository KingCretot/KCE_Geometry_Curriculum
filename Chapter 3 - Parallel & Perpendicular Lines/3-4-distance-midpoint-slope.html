Formula 1

The Distance Formula

Distance Between (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂)
d = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]

Where it comes from: this is just the Pythagorean Theorem in disguise. The horizontal gap and vertical gap between two points form the two legs of a right triangle — the distance between the points is that triangle's hypotenuse.

Formula 2

The Midpoint Formula

Midpoint of (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂)
M = ( (x₁+x₂)/2 , (y₁+y₂)/2 )
SEE IT ON A GRID

A(1, 2) and B(9, 10): M = ((1+9)/2, (2+10)/2) = (5, 6) — exactly halfway between the two points on both axes at once.

Formula 3

The Slope Formula — and what it reveals about parallel & perpendicular lines

Slope Between (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂)
m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁)

Parallel lines have the exact same slope: m₁ = m₂.

Perpendicular lines have slopes that are negative reciprocals of each other: m₁ · m₂ = −1. (Flip the fraction, flip the sign.)

Worked Example

All three formulas, one problem

Problem: Given C(2, 3) and D(8, 11), find the distance CD, the midpoint of CD, and the slope of line CD.

1
Distance
d = √[(8−2)² + (11−3)²] = √[36 + 64] = √100 = 10
2
Midpoint
M = ((2+8)/2, (3+11)/2) = (5, 7)
3
Slope
m = (11−3)/(8−2) = 8/6 = 4/3

Why This Matters

GPS, city planning, and every app with a map

Every mapping app calculating the straight-line distance between two addresses is running the Distance Formula behind the scenes. City planners checking whether a proposed new street stays parallel to the block grid, or crosses it at a true right angle, are running the exact slope comparison from this lesson.

Real world: video game developers use the Distance Formula constantly — every time a character needs to know "how far away is that enemy," the game engine is computing exactly this formula, dozens of times per second.
