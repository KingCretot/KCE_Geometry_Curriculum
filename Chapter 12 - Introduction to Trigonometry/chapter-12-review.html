Key Ideas · Lessons 12.1–12.2

Right-triangle trig

Lesson 12.1

SOH-CAH-TOA

For an acute angle θ: sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse, tan θ = opposite/adjacent. The ratios depend only on the angle, because every right triangle with that angle is similar by AA.

Example: Sides 7, 24, 25: for the angle opposite 7, tan = 7/24 and sin = 7/25.
Naming sides from angle θ
Lesson 12.2

Solving for sides and angles

Pick the ratio that links what you know to what you want, then solve. To find an angle, use an inverse: θ = sin⁻¹(…), cos⁻¹(…), or tan⁻¹(…). Angles of elevation and depression are measured from the horizontal and are equal (alternate interior angles).

Example: Hypotenuse 20, angle 30°: opposite = 20 sin 30° = 10.

Key Ideas · Lessons 12.3–12.5

Area, vectors, identities

Lesson 12.3

SAS area formula

A = ½ab sin C, using two sides and the angle between them. sin(180° − θ) = sin θ, so obtuse angles work too.

Example: Sides 10 and 7, included 30°: ½(10)(7)(0.5) = 17.5.
Lesson 12.4

Vectors

A vector has magnitude and direction. ⟨x, y⟩ has magnitude √(x² + y²). Add vectors component by component; a scalar multiplies each component. Magnitude r at angle θ: ⟨r cos θ, r sin θ⟩.

Example: ⟨−6, 8⟩ has magnitude 10. Magnitude 10 at 30°: ⟨8.66, 5⟩.
Lesson 12.5

Identities

tan θ = sin θ/cos θ. Reciprocals: csc = 1/sin, sec = 1/cos, cot = 1/tan. sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. Cofunctions: sin θ = cos(90° − θ).

Example: sin θ = 3/5 → cos θ = √(1 − 9/25) = 4/5.

Key Ideas · Lesson 12.6

Any triangle

Lesson 12.6

Laws of Sines and Cosines

Law of Sines: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C — use with a side and its opposite angle (AAS, ASA, SSA). Law of Cosines: c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C — use for SAS or SSS. SSA can give two triangles.

Example: a = 3, b = 8, C = 60°: c² = 9 + 64 − 24 = 49, c = 7.

Watch Out

The mistakes that cost the most points

The mistakeThe fix
Labeling opposite and adjacent from the wrong angleOpposite and adjacent depend on which angle you're using. The hypotenuse never changes.
Using sin when you need sin⁻¹Finding a side: sin, cos, tan. Finding an angle: the inverse functions.
Calculator in radian modeEvery answer in this course is in degrees. Check the mode before the test.
Using a non-included angle in ½ab sin CThe angle must be between the two sides.
Forgetting the −2ab cos CWithout it, the Law of Cosines is just the Pythagorean Theorem, which only works at 90°.
Mixing up a vector's componentsHorizontal is r cos θ; vertical is r sin θ.

Practice Test

10 questions, every lesson mixed together

Just like a unit test, these jump between lessons. You get two tries on each question, and every question explains itself after you answer.

Question 1
A right triangle has sides 7, 24, and 25. What is the tangent of the angle opposite the side of 7?
Here's why: tan = opposite/adjacent. The side opposite the angle is 7 and the other leg, 24, is adjacent: 7/24. 7/25 is the sine.
Question 2
A right triangle has a hypotenuse of 20 and a 30° angle. How long is the side opposite the 30° angle?
Here's why: sin 30° = opposite/20, so opposite = 20 × 0.5 = 10. 17.3 is 20 cos 30°, the adjacent side.
Question 3
A 15-ft ladder leans against a wall, making a 65° angle with the ground. About how high up the wall does it reach?
Here's why: The height is opposite the 65° angle and the ladder is the hypotenuse: 15 sin 65° ≈ 15(0.906) ≈ 13.6 ft. 6.3 ft uses cosine, which gives the distance from the wall.
Question 4
A right triangle has legs 5 and 12. To the nearest tenth, what is the angle opposite the side of 5?
Here's why: tan θ = 5/12, so θ = tan⁻¹(5/12) ≈ 22.6°. 67.4° is the other acute angle, and 24.6° uses sin⁻¹(5/12), treating 12 as the hypotenuse.
Question 5
A triangle has sides 10 and 7 with an included angle of 30°. What is its area?
Here's why: A = ½ab sin C = ½(10)(7)(0.5) = 17.5. 35 forgets the ½, and 30.3 uses cosine instead of sine.
Question 6
What is the magnitude of the vector ⟨−6, 8⟩?
Here's why: √((−6)² + 8²) = √(36 + 64) = √100 = 10. Squaring makes the negative positive.
Question 7
A vector has magnitude 10 and direction 30°. What is its vertical component?
Here's why: Vertical component = r sin θ = 10 sin 30° = 5. 8.66 is the horizontal component, 10 cos 30°.
Question 8
θ is acute and sin θ = 3/5. What is cos θ?
Here's why: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 → cos²θ = 1 − 9/25 = 16/25 → cos θ = 4/5. 3/4 is tan θ.
Question 9
Which is equal to cos 25°?
Here's why: Cofunction identity: cos θ = sin(90° − θ), so cos 25° = sin 65°.
Question 10
In △ABC, a = 3, b = 8, and C = 60°. What is c?
Here's why: Law of Cosines: c² = 3² + 8² − 2(3)(8)cos 60° = 9 + 64 − 24 = 49, so c = 7. 8.5 leaves out the −2ab cos C term.
