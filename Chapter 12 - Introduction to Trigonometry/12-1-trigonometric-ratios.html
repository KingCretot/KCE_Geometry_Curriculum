Let's Begin — Welcome to Chapter 12!

Ratios that measure the world

Trigonometry means “triangle measurement.” It connects the angles of a right triangle to the ratios of its sides. With it, you can find the height of a mountain or the width of a river from a single angle and one distance.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Name the opposite side, adjacent side, and hypotenuse for an acute angle
  • 2Find sine, cosine, and tangent as ratios
  • 3Explain why the ratios depend only on the angle

Vocabulary

Naming the sides from an angle

SIDES NAMED FROM ∠A

The hypotenuse is always across from the right angle. The other two sides depend on which acute angle you stand at.

The opposite side is across from the angle. The adjacent side touches the angle but is not the hypotenuse. Stand at the other acute angle, and opposite and adjacent trade places.

The Three Ratios

Sine, cosine, and tangent

Memory aid: SOH-CAH-TOA. Sine is Opposite over Hypotenuse, Cosine is Adjacent over Hypotenuse, Tangent is Opposite over Adjacent.

Worked Example

A 3-4-5 triangle

Problem: A right triangle has legs 3 and 4 and hypotenuse 5. ∠A is opposite the side of length 3. Find sin A, cos A, and tan A.

1
Label from ∠A
opposite = 3, adjacent = 4, hypotenuse = 5
2
Write the ratios
sin A = 3/5 = 0.6,   cos A = 4/5 = 0.8,   tan A = 3/4 = 0.75
3
Switch angles
For the other acute angle B, opposite and adjacent swap: sin B = 4/5, cos B = 3/5, tan B = 4/3.

Why It Works

Similar triangles make the ratios constant

Every right triangle with a 35° angle also has a 55° angle and a 90° angle, so all such triangles are similar by AA. Similar triangles have proportional sides, so the ratio opposite/hypotenuse is the same in every one of them.

That is why your calculator can give sin 35° as one number (about 0.5736) without knowing how big your triangle is. Be sure the calculator is in degree mode.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Use SOH-CAH-TOA.

Question 1 — A right triangle has sides 5, 12, and 13. Find the sine of the angle opposite the side of 5.

Here's why: sine = opposite/hypotenuse = 5/13. 12/13 is that angle’s cosine, and 5/12 is its tangent.

Question 2 — In the same triangle, find the tangent of the angle opposite the side of 12.

Here's why: From that angle, opposite = 12 and adjacent = 5, so tangent = 12/5.

Why This Matters

The math behind GPS, ramps, and roofs

A wheelchair ramp built to code rises at most 1 inch for every 12 inches of length, a tangent of 1/12, or an angle of about 4.8°. Roofers describe roof pitch the same way, such as a 6/12 pitch.

Real world: GPS receivers, video game engines, and engineers designing bridges all compute sines and cosines millions of times a second.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
A right triangle has sides 8, 15, and 17. What is the cosine of the angle opposite the side of 8?
Here's why: From that angle, adjacent = 15 and hypotenuse = 17, so cosine = 15/17. 8/17 is its sine.
Question 2
Tangent of an acute angle equals...
Here's why: TOA: Tangent is Opposite over Adjacent.
Question 3
What is sin 30°?
Here's why: In a 30-60-90 triangle, the side opposite 30° is half the hypotenuse. √3/2 is sin 60°.
Question 4
Why is sin 35° the same in every right triangle with a 35° angle?
Here's why: Same angles mean similar triangles, and similar triangles have proportional sides, so the ratio never changes.
Question 5 — True or False
The sine of an acute angle in a right triangle can be greater than 1.
Here's why: False. The hypotenuse is the longest side, so opposite/hypotenuse is always less than 1.
