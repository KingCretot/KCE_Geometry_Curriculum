Let's Begin

Size and direction together

Some quantities need only a size: a temperature of 72° or a mass of 5 kg. Others need a size and a direction: a wind blowing 20 mph from the north, or a force pushing up a ramp. These are vectors, and trigonometry is how we work with them.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Find the magnitude and direction of a vector
  • 2Add vectors and multiply a vector by a scalar
  • 3Break a vector into horizontal and vertical components

Vocabulary

Magnitude and direction

A vector is drawn as an arrow. Its length is its magnitude, and the way it points is its direction, usually given as an angle from the positive x-axis. In component form, ⟨x, y⟩ means x units horizontally and y units vertically.

THE VECTOR ⟨3, 4⟩

The arrow is the hypotenuse of a 3-4-5 right triangle, so its magnitude is 5.

Formulas

Magnitude and direction from components

Magnitude of ⟨x, y⟩
|v| = √(x² + y²)
Direction (first quadrant)
θ = tan−1(y ÷ x)
1
Example
⟨3, 4⟩: |v| = √(9 + 16) = 5, and θ = tan−1(4/3) ≈ 53.1°

A vector from A(x1, y1) to B(x2, y2) has components ⟨x2 − x1, y2 − y1⟩: end minus start.

Operations

Adding and scaling

Add vectors by adding their components: ⟨2, 5⟩ + ⟨4, −1⟩ = ⟨6, 4⟩. Geometrically, place them tip-to-tail; the sum, or resultant, runs from the first tail to the last tip.

Multiply a vector by a number (a scalar) by multiplying each component: 3⟨2, −1⟩ = ⟨6, −3⟩. The magnitude triples, and the direction stays the same.

Components

Breaking a vector apart

Components of a vector with magnitude r at angle θ
⟨r cos θ, r sin θ⟩

Problem: A force of 10 newtons pulls at 30° above horizontal. Find its horizontal and vertical components.

1
Horizontal
10 cos 30° ≈ 10(0.866) = 8.66 N
2
Vertical
10 sin 30° = 10(0.5) = 5 N

Worked Example

A plane in the wind

Problem: A plane flies east at 300 mph while a wind blows north at 40 mph. Find its actual speed and direction.

1
Add the vectors
⟨300, 0⟩ + ⟨0, 40⟩ = ⟨300, 40⟩
2
Speed
√(300² + 40²) = √91,600 ≈ 302.7 mph
3
Direction
tan−1(40/300) ≈ 7.6° north of east

Why This Matters

Flight, sports, and physics

Pilots and boat captains constantly add vectors: the craft’s own velocity plus the wind or current gives the actual path over the ground. To fly due east in a north wind, a pilot must aim slightly south.

Real world: a quarterback’s pass, a soccer free kick, and the forces on a bridge cable are all analyzed by splitting vectors into horizontal and vertical components.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
What is the magnitude of ⟨5, 12⟩?
Here's why: √(25 + 144) = √169 = 13. 17 adds the components, and 169 forgets the square root.
Question 2
What is 3⟨2, −1⟩?
Here's why: Multiply each component by 3: ⟨6, −3⟩. ⟨6, −1⟩ scales only the first.
Question 3
A vector has magnitude 20 and direction 60°. What is its horizontal component?
Here's why: 20 cos 60° = 20(0.5) = 10. 17.3 is the vertical component, 20 sin 60°.
Question 4
What is the component form of the vector from A(1, 2) to B(4, 6)?
Here's why: End minus start: ⟨4 − 1, 6 − 2⟩ = ⟨3, 4⟩. ⟨−3, −4⟩ goes from B back to A.
Question 5 — True or False
Two vectors with the same magnitude and direction are equal, even if they start at different points.
Here's why: True. A vector is defined by its size and direction, not by where it is drawn.
