Let's Begin

The final lesson of Chapter 7: circles, sectors, and segments

We close out Chapter 7 with the one shape that has no straight sides at all: the circle. We'll find the area of a full circle, then a "slice" of it (a sector), and finally the region left over when a chord cuts a piece off (a segment).

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Calculate the area of a circle
  • 2Calculate the area of a sector
  • 3Calculate the area of a segment

Core Formula

Area of a circle

Circle Area
A = πr²

Pi (π) times the radius squared. This is the same π you already know from circumference (C = 2πr) — the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter, approximately 3.14159. Remember to square the radius, not the diameter.

Core Formula

Area of a sector

Sector Area
A = (θ/360) × πr²
A SECTOR IS A "PIE SLICE" OF THE CIRCLE

A sector is a pie-slice-shaped region bounded by two radii and an arc. Since it's only a fraction of the full circle, take the sector's central angle (θ) out of the full 360°, then multiply that fraction by the full circle's area.

Core Formula

Area of a segment

Segment Area
A(segment) = A(sector) − A(triangle)

A segment is the region between a chord and the arc it cuts off — it's what remains after you remove the triangular piece from a sector. Find the sector's area, then subtract the area of the triangle formed by the two radii and the chord.

Worked Example

Applying all three formulas

Problem: A circle has radius 6. (a) Find its area. (b) Find the area of a 90° sector.

1
Full circle area
A = π(6)² = 36π ≈ 113.1 square units
2
90° sector area
A = (90/360) × 36π = ¼ × 36π = 9π ≈ 28.3 square units

Why This Matters

Pizza slices, pie charts, and irrigation

Sector area is exactly how a pizza shop calculates the surface of each slice, how a data analyst sizes wedges in a pie chart, and how a farmer using a center-pivot irrigation system calculates how much of a circular field a given rotation angle waters.

Real world: segment area calculations are used in engineering to find the cross-sectional area of liquid inside a partially-filled cylindrical tank — the liquid's surface forms a segment shape when viewed from the end.
