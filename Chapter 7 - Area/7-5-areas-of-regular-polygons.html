Let's Begin

Beyond four sides: pentagons, hexagons, and beyond

Every formula so far in this chapter has covered triangles and quadrilaterals. Today we extend area calculations to any regular polygon — a shape with all sides and all angles congruent, like a regular pentagon, hexagon, or octagon — using a single unified formula.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Identify the center, radius, and apothem of a regular polygon
  • 2Calculate the area of a regular polygon
  • 3Apply the formula to polygons with different numbers of sides

Core Vocabulary

Center, radius, and apothem

A REGULAR HEXAGON, WITH ITS CENTER MARKED

The center is the point equidistant from every vertex. The radius connects the center to a vertex (the gold dashed line). The apothem connects the center to the midpoint of a side, perpendicular to that side (the red dashed line) — the apothem is what the area formula actually uses.

Core Formula

Area of a regular polygon

Regular Polygon Area
A = ½ × a × P

Half the apothem (a) times the perimeter (P). This works because a regular polygon can be split into n congruent isosceles triangles from its center, each with height equal to the apothem — summing those triangle areas simplifies directly to this formula, no matter how many sides the polygon has.

Worked Example

Applying the formula

Problem: A regular hexagon has side length 8 and apothem 4√3. Find its area.

1
Find the perimeter
A regular hexagon has 6 congruent sides: P = 6 × 8 = 48
2
Apply the formula
A = ½ × 4√3 × 48
3
Simplify
A = 96√3 square units

Core Concept

The formula works for every regular polygon

The exact same A = ½aP formula applies whether the polygon has 5 sides, 6 sides, or 20 sides — only the perimeter calculation changes based on how many sides there are and how long each one is. The apothem, however, must always be found or given separately; it usually requires the 30-60-90 or other special-triangle relationships from Lesson 7.4 for regular hexagons, since a regular hexagon's center-to-vertex triangles are always equilateral.

Why This Matters

Stop signs, gazebos, and honeycomb structures

A stop sign is a regular octagon, a gazebo floor is often a regular hexagon, and honeycomb cells are regular hexagons too — nature and manufacturing both favor regular polygons because they tile efficiently and distribute material or structural load evenly from the center outward.

Real world: architects calculating the material needed for a hexagonal or octagonal gazebo floor, patio, or pavilion use exactly this apothem-and-perimeter formula.
