Key Ideas · Lessons 7.1–7.2

Polygon area formulas

Lesson 7.1

Base times perpendicular height

Rectangle and parallelogram: A = bh. Triangle: A = ½bh. The height is always perpendicular to the base — never the slanted side.

Example: A parallelogram with base 12, slanted side 7, and height 5 has A = 12 × 5 = 60.
Lesson 7.2

Trapezoids, rhombi, and kites

Trapezoid: A = ½h(b₁ + b₂). Rhombus or kite: A = ½d₁d₂, using the two diagonals (which are perpendicular).

Example: Bases 5 and 9, height 6: ½(6)(14) = 42.

Key Ideas · Lessons 7.3–7.4

Right triangles

Lesson 7.3

Pythagorean Theorem and its converse

In a right triangle, a² + b² = c², where c is the hypotenuse. If a² + b² = c² for a triangle's sides, it's right; if c² is bigger it's obtuse; if smaller, acute.

Example: 6, 8, 11: 36 + 64 = 100 < 121, so not right (obtuse).
Lesson 7.4

Special right triangles

45-45-90: legs x and x, hypotenuse x√2. 30-60-90: short leg x, long leg x√3, hypotenuse 2x. The short leg is opposite the 30° angle.

Example: Hypotenuse 14 in a 30-60-90: short leg 7, long leg 7√3.

Key Ideas · Lessons 7.5–7.6

Regular polygons and circles

Lesson 7.5

Apothem and perimeter

The apothem runs from the center to the midpoint of a side, perpendicular to it. A = ½aP, where P is the perimeter. The radius runs from the center to a vertex.

Example: Regular hexagon, side 8: P = 48, apothem 4√3, A = ½(4√3)(48) = 96√3.
Lesson 7.6

Circles, sectors, segments

Circle: A = πr². Sector: A = (θ/360)πr². Segment (between a chord and its arc) = sector area − triangle area.

Example: 120° sector, radius 6: (120/360)(36π) = 12π.

Watch Out

The mistakes that cost the most points

The mistakeThe fix
Using the slanted side as the heightHeight is perpendicular to the base. In a parallelogram, the slanted side is longer than the height.
Forgetting the ½ in triangle, trapezoid, and kite formulasTriangle ½bh, trapezoid ½h(b₁ + b₂), kite ½d₁d₂.
Putting the hypotenuse in for a or bc is always the side across from the right angle, the longest side.
Adding when you should subtract for a missing legLeg² = hypotenuse² − other leg². Hypotenuse 25, leg 7: √(625 − 49) = 24.
Mixing up √2 and √3√2 goes with 45-45-90. √3 goes with 30-60-90, on the long leg.
Using the radius as the apothemThe apothem goes to the middle of a side; the radius goes to a vertex.
Using diameter in πr²Halve the diameter first.

Practice Test

10 questions, every lesson mixed together

Just like a unit test, these jump between lessons. You get two tries on each question, and every question explains itself after you answer.

Question 1
A parallelogram has base 12, slanted side 7, and height 5. What is its area?
Here's why: A = bh uses the perpendicular height, not the slanted side: 12 × 5 = 60. 84 uses the slanted side instead.
Question 2
What is the area of a triangle with base 14 and height 9?
Here's why: A = ½bh = ½(14)(9) = 63. 126 forgets the ½.
Question 3
A trapezoid has bases 7 and 13 and height 8. What is its area?
Here's why: A = ½h(b₁ + b₂) = ½(8)(20) = 80. 160 forgets the ½, and 56 and 104 use just one base.
Question 4
A kite has diagonals of 10 and 16. What is its area?
Here's why: A = ½d₁d₂ = ½(10)(16) = 80. 160 forgets the ½, and 26 adds the diagonals.
Question 5
A right triangle has legs 9 and 12. What is the hypotenuse?
Here's why: c² = 81 + 144 = 225, so c = 15. 225 forgets the square root, and 21 adds the legs.
Question 6
A right triangle has hypotenuse 25 and one leg 7. What is the other leg?
Here's why: Leg² = 25² − 7² = 625 − 49 = 576, so the leg is 24. 26 adds the squares as if 25 were a leg.
Question 7
A 45-45-90 triangle has legs of 6. What is the hypotenuse?
Here's why: In a 45-45-90 triangle, hypotenuse = leg × √2 = 6√2 (about 8.49). √3 belongs to the 30-60-90 triangle.
Question 8
A 30-60-90 triangle has hypotenuse 14. What is the longer leg?
Here's why: Hypotenuse = 2x, so x = 7 is the short leg. The long leg is x√3 = 7√3 (about 12.1).
Question 9
A regular hexagon has side 8 and apothem 4√3. What is its area?
Here's why: P = 6 × 8 = 48. A = ½aP = ½(4√3)(48) = 96√3. 192√3 forgets the ½.
Question 10
What is the area of a 120° sector of a circle with radius 6?
Here's why: The full circle is π(6²) = 36π. A 120° sector is 120/360 = 1/3 of it: 12π. 4π is one-third of the circumference — that's the arc length, not the area.
