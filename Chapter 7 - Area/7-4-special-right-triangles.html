Let's Begin

Two shortcuts that skip the Pythagorean Theorem entirely

Two specific right triangles show up constantly in geometry: the 45-45-90 triangle (half a square) and the 30-60-90 triangle (half an equilateral triangle). Both have fixed side-length ratios, so once you know just one side, you instantly know the other two — no Pythagorean Theorem required.

  • 1Apply the 45-45-90 Triangle Theorem
  • 2Apply the 30-60-90 Triangle Theorem

Core Theorem

The 45-45-90 Triangle Theorem

45-45-90 Ratio
leg : leg : hypotenuse = x : x : x√2
HALF OF A SQUARE, CUT ALONG THE DIAGONAL

A 45-45-90 triangle is an isosceles right triangle — exactly half of a square split along its diagonal. Both legs are equal (call each one x), and the hypotenuse is always x√2, the leg length times the square root of 2.

Core Theorem

The 30-60-90 Triangle Theorem

30-60-90 Ratio
short leg : long leg : hypotenuse = x : x√3 : 2x

A 30-60-90 triangle is exactly half of an equilateral triangle, split by an altitude. The shortest side (opposite the 30° angle) is x. The longer leg (opposite 60°) is x√3. The hypotenuse (opposite the right angle, and also the original equilateral triangle's side) is 2x — always twice the shortest side.

Core Concept

Recognizing which triangle you have

Both special-right-triangle theorems only apply when you already know the triangle is 45-45-90 or 30-60-90 — usually because the problem states the angle measures directly, or because the triangle came from cutting a square or equilateral triangle in half. If you're not sure which triangle you have, the ordinary Pythagorean Theorem from Lesson 7.3 always works as a fallback.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

A 45-45-90 triangle has a hypotenuse of 10√2.

Question 1 — What is the length of each leg?

Here's why: In a 45-45-90 triangle, hypotenuse = leg × √2. If 10√2 = leg × √2, the leg is 10.

Question 2 — A 30-60-90 triangle has a hypotenuse of 16. What is the short leg?

Here's why: In a 30-60-90 triangle, hypotenuse = 2 × short leg, so the short leg is 16 ÷ 2 = 8. 16√3 multiplies when it should divide.

Why This Matters

Ramps, roof pitches, and engineering shortcuts

Structural engineers and framers rely on these fixed ratios constantly — a standard 45° roof pitch or a 30°/60° ramp angle means a builder can instantly calculate every dimension from just one measurement, without pulling out a calculator for square roots each time.

Real world: the 30-60-90 triangle is the reason equilateral-triangle trusses (common in bridge and roof design) can be split into two identical, easily-calculated right-triangle halves.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
A 45-45-90 triangle's hypotenuse is always...
Here's why: Hypotenuse = leg × √2. A 45-45-90 triangle is half of a square, and a square's diagonal is always its side times √2.
Question 2
A 30-60-90 triangle's hypotenuse is always...
Here's why: Hypotenuse = 2 × short leg. A 30-60-90 triangle is half of an equilateral triangle, so the short leg is half of a full side.
Question 3
A 45-45-90 triangle is exactly half of a...
Here's why: Cut a square along one diagonal and you get two 45-45-90 triangles. Half of an equilateral triangle is a 30-60-90.
Question 4
A 30-60-90 triangle's longer leg equals...
Here's why: Long leg = short leg × √3. Doubling the short leg gives the hypotenuse, not the long leg.
Question 5 — True or False
If you're unsure whether a triangle fits either special ratio, the ordinary Pythagorean Theorem still works.
Here's why: True. The special ratios are shortcuts, and the Pythagorean Theorem works on every right triangle. If you forget a ratio, you can always fall back on it.
