Let's Begin

Give a flat shape some depth, and it becomes a solid.

Last week's polygons were completely flat. Today we lift them off the page — stack them, connect them, close them up — and build actual solids: boxes, pyramids, the shapes that fill three-dimensional space.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Identify the faces, edges, and vertices of a solid
  • 2Tell a prism apart from a pyramid

Vocabulary

Three parts every solid is made of

Polyhedron

A polyhedron is a 3-D solid whose surfaces are all flat polygons — no curves anywhere (a sphere or a cylinder is NOT a polyhedron).

Face — a flat polygon surface Vertex — a corner point

Where two faces meet is an edge — a straight segment, shown by the lines between the colored faces above.

Solid Type 1

Prism — two matching ends, connected straight across

Prism

A prism has two parallel, congruent polygon bases, connected by rectangles (or parallelograms). The bases give it its name — a rectangular prism has rectangle bases, a triangular prism has triangle bases.

Solid Type 2

Pyramid — one base, all sides climb to a point

Pyramid

A pyramid has one polygon base, with triangular faces that all rise up and meet at a single point — the apex. Like prisms, its name comes from the base shape (a square pyramid, a triangular pyramid).

A Rule That Always Works

Euler's Formula

V − E + F = 2

Vertices − Edges + Faces always equals 2, for any solid without holes through it.

Mathematician Leonhard Euler noticed this pattern holds for every convex polyhedron — cubes, pyramids, soccer balls, all of it. It's a quick way to double-check you've counted a solid's parts correctly.

Worked Example

Checking a cube with Euler's Formula

Problem: A cube has 8 vertices and 6 faces. How many edges should it have?

1
Start with the formula
V − E + F = 2
2
Substitute what we know
8 − E + 6 = 2
3
Solve for E
14 − E = 2, so E = 12
4
Check it makes sense
A cube does have 12 edges — 4 on top, 4 on bottom, 4 connecting them. It checks out!

Why This Matters

Every box, tent, and building you've ever seen is one of these

  • Cardboard shipping boxes are rectangular prisms — picked because they stack with zero wasted space.
  • A camping tent is often shaped like a triangular prism.
  • The Louvre Pyramid in Paris and ancient Egyptian pyramids are, unsurprisingly, pyramids.
