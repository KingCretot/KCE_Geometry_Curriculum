Let's Begin

A line goes forever. Most of the time, we only need a piece of it.

A fence between two posts. A flashlight beam cutting through the dark. A hallway stretching away from where you stand. None of those go on forever — but they're all built from the same idea as a line. Today we name the pieces.

Last week's postulates were all about full, endless lines. Today we cut lines down into the shapes that actually show up in real diagrams: segments and rays.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Define and name a segment and a ray using correct notation
  • 2Identify opposite rays
  • 3Use betweenness to describe the order of points on a line

Piece 1 of 2

Segment — a line with two ends

•—•

Segment

A segment is the part of a line between two points, including those two points. Unlike a full line, it stops at both ends — it has a measurable length.

Written as: segment AB, with a bar over “AB” and no arrows

Piece 2 of 2

Ray — one end, forever in the other direction

•→

Ray

A ray starts at one point (the endpoint) and goes on forever in one direction only. Think of a flashlight beam — it starts right at the flashlight and never stops going the other way.

Written as: ray CD, with a single arrow over “CD” — order matters! The endpoint is always listed first.

A Special Pair

Opposite rays — two rays, one straight line

Opposite rays share the same endpoint and point in exactly opposite directions. Put them together and they rebuild a full straight line.

Ray PA (blue, pointing left) and ray PB (red, pointing right) share endpoint P and point opposite ways — they're opposite rays.

Putting Points in Order

Betweenness — which point sits in the middle?

Point B is between A and C when all three points are collinear, and B sits on the segment connecting A and C. When that's true, the pieces add up: AB + BC = AC.

B is between A and C, so AB + BC = AC.

Worked Example

Using betweenness to find a missing length

Problem: Point Y is between X and Z. XY = 9 and YZ = 6. Find XZ.

1
Which rule applies?
Since Y is between X and Z, we know XY + YZ = XZ.
2
Substitute the known values
9 + 6 = XZ
3
Solve
XZ = 15

Why This Matters

Segments and rays are what real diagrams are made of

You will almost never draw an entire infinite line in a real problem — you'll draw segments (the sides of shapes) and rays (the sides of angles). Today's vocabulary is what lets you talk about those pieces precisely.

Around you right now

  • A fence rail between two posts is a segment.
  • A flashlight beam or a sunbeam is a ray.
  • Standing in a hallway and looking both ways gives you a pair of opposite rays.
