Let's Begin

Now that we can name the pieces, let's measure them.

A ruler turns a segment into a number. A protractor turns an angle into a number. Today we make that official with two more postulates — and learn the addition rules that let us find measurements we can't directly see.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Use the Ruler Postulate to find the distance between two points
  • 2Use Segment and Angle Addition to find missing measurements
  • 3Classify an angle as acute, right, obtuse, straight, or reflex

Measuring Segments

The Ruler Postulate

RULER POSTULATE

Every point on a line matches up with exactly one real number, called its coordinate. The distance between two points is the absolute value of the difference between their coordinates.

AB = |9 − 2| = 7

Adding the Pieces

Segment Addition Postulate

If B is between A and C, then AB + BC = AC. You saw this last week — now it's official as a postulate, ready to use in proofs.

Measuring Angles

The Protractor Postulate

PROTRACTOR POSTULATE

Given a ray, every other ray from that same endpoint can be matched to a number between 0° and 180° — its angle measure.

Five Kinds of Angles

Classifying angles by their measure

Acute
less than 90°
Right
exactly 90°
Obtuse
between 90° & 180°
Straight
exactly 180°
Reflex
more than 180°

Worked Example

Angle Addition Postulate in action

Problem: Ray OB splits angle AOC into two parts. Angle AOB = 25° and angle BOC = 38°. Find angle AOC.

1
Which rule applies?
Angle Addition Postulate: if B is inside angle AOC, then angle AOB + angle BOC = angle AOC.
2
Substitute
25° + 38° = angle AOC
3
Solve
angle AOC = 63° — and since it's less than 90°, it's acute.

Why This Matters

Measurement is what turns geometry into something you can check

Every construction crew, architect, and carpenter relies on exactly these two postulates — a tape measure is the Ruler Postulate in your hands, and a protractor (or a digital angle finder) is the Protractor Postulate.

Real world: a roofer cutting rafters needs the exact angle where two boards meet — get it wrong by even a couple degrees and the whole roofline is off. Angle Addition is exactly how that angle often gets calculated from two easier-to-measure pieces.
